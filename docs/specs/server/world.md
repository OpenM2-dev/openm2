# Server: the world

What lives in openm2's own server's world besides the players: the monsters and NPCs of its content folder ([`content.md`](content.md)), how they wake, stroll, fight, die, come back and sleep, and what the players around them see; the players' points, fights, deaths and levels; their items, and items on the ground; NPCs' shops; skills. The session around it (logging in, entering, moving, chat) is in [`session.md`](session.md).

Each rule says where it comes from, as in `session.md`: **QCX** (Quantum Core X `007c7a6` does the same: its source and captures of sessions against it) or **openm2** (our own choice). The messages are openm2's ([`../protocol/openm2/`](../protocol/openm2/README.md)).

**Checked by:** `crates/m2-server-core/tests/world.rs`, `packs.rs`, `stones.rs`, `shooting.rs`, `walls.rs`, `combat.rs`, `items.rs`, `shops.rs` and `skills.rs` (snapshots beside them), the rules in `random_sessions.rs` (a vid is moved, described, hurt, killed or removed only between its spawn and its removal, ground items alike; no bag ever holds two items in one place, as its client sees it or as stored; gold never past its most; fights, items, shops and skills included), real clients in `crates/m2-server/tests/clients.rs`, and the bot in `crates/m2-cli/tests/own_server.rs` (it watches monsters stroll, kills one, dies and restarts, wears, drops and picks up items, shops, uses a skill, and does all of it in one run).

## Entities and vids
- When the server starts, every group's monsters and every NPC of the content take their place and a vid, before anyone logs in: groups in file order, then NPCs. Players take the next vids when they select a character, items on the ground when they land (QCX: one counter for players, monsters and items); a vid is free again once what held it is gone.
- **Where a group's monsters stand:** each at a random point of its area (its circle or rectangle) on an open square (openm2; QCX picks a random point in its spawn rectangle; "Walls"). They face the group's `rotation`, else a random direction.
- **Packs** ([`content.md`](content.md), "Pack"): a group of `count` packs is that many groups of the server, each a pack of its own. Its leader stands at a random point of the group's area, the others at random points within **5 m** of it (openm2: QCX puts each monster of a group at its own random point of the spawn rectangle, then up to 5 m from it). That circle of 5 m around the leader's point is the pack's area until it comes back ("A monster's death"). Monsters are placed in the order of the files, a pack's leader first and then its members as written, each taking the next vid.
- A monster keeps its vid when it dies and comes back (openm2: QCX gives it a new one).
- The world's random numbers (points, directions, strolls, damage) come from the server's seed on a stream of their own, so they never change the tickets.

## View
- A player starts seeing what comes within **100 m** of it (10,000 units, QCX's view distance) and keeps seeing it until it is more than **120 m** away (openm2: QCX has no margin; without one, a player standing or fighting at the edge sees an NPC come and go with every swing). A player is measured from where it is: when it moves, then on the server's tick every **5 m** of a walk and where the walk ends (openm2; QCX measures where everyone is on each tick). Monsters are measured from where their walk ends: they walk at most 7 m, so the difference lasts a second or two at the edge. Measuring players at their walk's end too would take monsters away from a player running past them towards a point beyond the margin, and show the monsters near that point while the player is still far.
- What a player is shown when a monster or NPC comes into view (QCX's order):
  - **EntitySpawn** {vid, angle in degrees (its direction × 5), where it is now, z 0, kind from the content, race = its number, its movement and attack speed, state 0, effects 0};
  - **EntityInfo** for NPCs only: {vid, name, no parts, empire 0, guild 0, level 0} (QCX sends none for monsters). Names go out as the content has them, control characters as `?`, at most 64 bytes (openm2);
  - if it is walking: **EntityMove** {vid, kind 1, argument 0, its angle, where the walk ends, the milliseconds left} (openm2), so it carries on from where the player first sees it.
- A dead monster is not shown to players who come later (QCX); a dead player is, lying (state 1, openm2).
- Going out of view sends **EntityRemove**, and a player looking at what goes stops looking at it. Leaving the game sends nothing to the monsters, which only stop being watched.
- Entering the game shows the players around first, then the monsters and NPCs, then the items on the ground, by vid.
- Items on the ground follow the same view rules from where they lie (The ground).
- Who is near is looked up in a grid of square cells as wide as the view radius, players where they last looked around, monsters where their walk ends, so nothing scans the whole world (openm2).

## Sleeping and waking
- A group is **awake** while a player (where it last looked around, as for the view) is within 120 m plus the group's reach of its centre (its radius, half a rectangle's diagonal, a pack's 5 m: as far as its monsters can stay in view), and asleep otherwise (openm2: QCX updates every monster of every map 100 times a second). Players count from the moment they enter, dead or alive.
- Asleep, its idle monsters do nothing and cost nothing: a world without players runs no monster code at all. Its fighters give up and walk home; a walk under way still ends where it was going; the dead are still removed and the group still comes back on time.
- Waking gives each idle monster its first stroll at a random time within the longest stroll interval, so they do not all move at once.
- NPCs never stroll.

## Walls
- A map's walk mask ([`content.md`](content.md), "Walk masks") holds for everyone on the map (openm2). A map without one is open everywhere, and so is a point on no map (a start off the maps).
- **Players:** a move that leaves its map, crosses a blocked square or is out of reach is refused ([`session.md`](session.md), "Moving").
- **Where a group's monsters stand:** a random point of the area on a blocked square is drawn again (16 tries in all, then the centre); a pack's point too, and its members' around it.
- **Strolls** take only directions with a clear line, and so does the way back towards the centre; with none, the monster does not stroll that time ("Strolling").
- **Chases** go straight to just short of the target when that line is clear, else along the first leg of a path around the blocked squares (`WalkMap::find_path`), its last leg ending short of the target. Each step of the fight looks again; a monster already walking to the same point walks on. A monster with no path to its target gives it up ("Monsters fighting back"). It bites only when no blocked square lies between it and its target.
- **Going home** follows the path back a leg at a time; a monster with no way home is idle where it stands.
- Slopes, water that is not blocked and bodies do not stop anyone.

## Strolling
- An awake, idle monster strolls every **10 to 20 s** (a random time each, QCX), **3 to 7 m** (QCX) in a random direction (one of the protocol's 72), **inside its group's area** (a pack's: 5 m around its leader's point) and clear of walls (openm2: QCX lets monsters wander off). It does not stroll while it fights or walks home (openm2: QCX's do).
  - It tries 4 directions; if none stays inside with a clear line, it heads for the centre, at most the distance to it, unless a wall is in the way.
  - Groups narrower than 1 m (a circle's radius, or half a rectangle's narrower side), monsters with `moves = false`, and anything that is not a `monster` (stones, NPCs) never stroll.
  - A stroll starts from where the monster is, mid-walk too.
- The players that see it get **EntityMove** {vid, kind 1, argument 0, angle, the end point, duration} (openm2: QCX sends kind 0 and, without motion data, duration 0; [`movement.md`](../protocol/openm2/movement.md)).
- **Duration:** distance / run speed × 1000, whole milliseconds, × the movement speed's percent / 100, the same rule as players' walks (QCX's rule, [`movement.md`](../protocol/openm2/movement.md), "Speed"). The run speed is the monster's `run_speed` from the content (the import takes it from the run motion `play` draws it with), else 4.5 m/s.
- Players who come within 100 m of where it ends see it appear (as above); players now farther than 120 m see it go.
- Directions and distances use a table of the 72 directions and exactly rounded square roots, so the numbers are the same on every platform (openm2).
- `Config::wander_every` and `Config::wander_step` hold the two intervals.

## Players' walks
- A player's walk is timed with its race's `run_speed` from `races.toml` (the import takes it from `general/run.msa`), else 4.5 m/s ([`session.md`](session.md), "Moving").

## Points
A player's points ([`entities.md`](../protocol/openm2/entities.md), "Points"): all of those that are not 0 in Points while it loads, then only those that changed in PointsChanged (openm2: QCX sent all 255 every time):

| Point | What | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | level | |
| 3, 4 | experience, and what the next level needs | 0 and 0 at the highest level |
| 5, 6 | health, maximum health | start health + 40 × vitality + 36 × level (QCX) |
| 7, 8 | mana, maximum mana | start mana + 20 × intelligence + 44 × level (QCX) |
| 11 | gold | at most 2,000,000,000 (`Config::max_gold`, openm2) |
| 12 to 15 | strength, vitality, dexterity, intelligence | |
| 16, 20 | defence | level + vitality × 4 / 5, rounded down, + the worn armour's (Gear) (QCX), + what skills raise (openm2) |
| 17, 19 | attack speed, movement speed | 100 and 150 (QCX), changed by the worn items (Gear) and skills (openm2) |
| 18 | attack grade | 2 × level + 2 × strength (openm2: QCX's rule for monsters; its players have 0), + what skills raise (openm2) |
| 26, 27, 28 | status points, sub-skill points, skill points | sub-skill points 1 |
| 31 | play time in minutes | |

- Start health and mana by job (QCX): warrior 600/200, ninja 650/200, sura 650/200, shaman 700/200.
- **Status points** come with level-ups (Experience and levels). **RaiseStatus** {point} spends one on strength (12), vitality (13), dexterity (14) or intelligence (15): +1 to the stat, up to 90 (`Config::max_stat`, openm2). The maxima, defence and attack grade follow it at once, without healing; the player gets PointsChanged. Refusals get a line in the chat: no status points left, the stat at its highest. Another point number is ignored; outside the game the message closes the connection.
- The maxima follow the level: they are worked out again at each level-up (openm2: QCX's stay as they were at login).
- Health and mana are kept with the character ([`storage.md`](storage.md)); health 0 is dead.

## Fighting
### Targets
- **SetTarget** with the vid of a player or monster the player sees: it looks at it and gets **TargetHealth** {vid, health in percent of the maximum, rounded down} at once (QCX). A monster's or stone's own health; a player's own health (openm2: QCX always says 100); 100 for what cannot be fought. An item on the ground is not a target (openm2).
- SetTarget 0 looks at nothing (openm2); a vid the player does not see is ignored and the old target stays (QCX).
- A player stops looking at what goes out of its view.
- After every hit something takes, every player looking at it gets TargetHealth again (QCX).

### Hits
The client leads its attacks ([`combat.md`](../protocol/openm2/combat.md)): it plays the motion and sends **Hit** {vid, 0} when a hit lands. The server checks every hit (openm2: QCX checks none of this); a hit that breaks a rule is ignored, without an answer:
- the player is in game and alive;
- `skill` is 0; a skill's hits have rules of their own (Skills, below);
- the vid is one the player sees, and a monster or stone that lives (no fights between players, and a corpse takes no hits: openm2; QCX pays a corpse's experience again for every hit);
- the player and its target, where each is when the hit arrives, are at most **5 m** apart (`Config::attack_reach`);
- the player's last hit on that target was at least **150 ms** before (`Config::hit_gap`). `play`'s sword combo hits a monster every 0.9 s or more in our captures.

A hit that counts: **Damage** {vid, 1, amount} to the attacker, TargetHealth to everyone looking at the target, and the target reacts (below) or dies.

### Damage
QCX's rule, in whole thousandths (openm2) so every platform gets the same numbers:
1. **Hit rating** of each side: (4 × dexterity + 2 × level) / 6, at most 90.
2. **What lands**, in thousandths: (attacker's rating + 210) / 300 − 0.3 × (2 × victim's rating + 5) / (victim's rating + 95).
3. **Attack:** (grade + 2 × roll − 2 × level) × what lands / 1000, rounded down, + 2 × level.
   - A player's grade is its attack grade (Points, with what skills raise); its roll is a random number from its worn weapon's least to most damage (values 3 and 4), and twice the weapon's bonus (value 5) is added after the 2 × level (QCX). Without a weapon its fists count as one of **strength to 2 × strength** with no bonus (openm2: QCX's players without a weapon hit for 1 to 5).
   - A monster's grade is 2 × level + 2 × strength, its roll a random number in its `damage` range; its attack is then multiplied by its `damage_multiplier`.
4. **Defence** is taken off: a monster's is its level + vitality + `defence`, a player's its defence point (with its armour's). Below 0 is 0.
5. Damage under 3 becomes a random 1 to 5.

No critical or piercing hits (QCX never rolls them).

## Monsters fighting back
- **Who they fight:** a monster that is hit fights the player who hit it if it fights no one, or if that player has now done it more damage than its target has (QCX). The idle monsters of its group join in against the same player (QCX).
- **Aggressive monsters** (`aggressive`) turn on the nearest living player not hidden ("Skill effects") within their `aggressive_sight` (else 10 m, at most 100 m), when that player enters, moves, walks into it (looked at every 5 m) or comes back to life there, or when the monster strolls, gets home or comes back to the world near it (openm2: QCX takes the first player within 100 m). Distances are from where the player is, as for the view, to where the monster's walk ends.
- A fight begins at the next tick and goes in steps every **300 ms** (`Config::chase_every`):
  - **chasing:** out of its reach (its `attack_range`, at least 1 m), it walks to 0.8 × its reach short of where its target is, again whenever its target is out of reach of where its walk ends (QCX's rule, in steps), around walls ("Walls"). Monsters with `moves = false` do not chase;
  - **biting:** within its reach (and 0.5 m more, for the target's last steps), it bites every **2 s** × 100 / its attack speed (QCX: every 2 s). Within reach on its way, it stops where it is first (openm2: QCX's monsters bite only standing, and pass a player coming towards them between two steps).
- **A bite:** after what the player's skills raise ("Skill effects": dodging, resistance, a mana shield), Damage {the player's vid, 1, amount} and PointsChanged to the player, TargetHealth to those looking at the player, then **EntityMove** {vid, kind 2, argument 0, its angle towards the player, where it stands, 0} to the players that see the monster (QCX's order). A stop without a bite is EntityMove kind 0 where it stands (openm2).
- **Shooting:** a monster of `battle` 1 (range, QCX) or 2 (magic: openm2, QCX doesn't handle it yet) bites from its reach as any, and after each bite's EntityMove the players that see it get **Projectile** {its vid, the player's vid, where the player is} (QCX sends it before the damage): their client flies its attack motion's flying effect there. The damage doesn't wait for the shot to arrive (QCX).
- **Giving up** (QCX): its target died, left the game, or is going to a point 40 m or more away (where its walk ends: openm2), or no path leads to it (openm2); or 15 s have passed since it was last hit (or its fight began) and its target is out of its reach or the monster is 50 m from where it was last hit. It then fights the player who did it the most damage that is in game, alive and within 40 m, else walks back to where its fight began (QCX: to where it appeared).
- **States:** a stunned monster takes no step of its fight, nor strolls, until it ends; a slowed one walks slower ("Skill effects").
- **Healing:** out of fights it regains its `hp_regen` percent of its health every `hp_regen` seconds (openm2: QCX's monsters never heal).

## A monster's death
- When a hit takes its last health: Damage, TargetHealth 0 and **EntityDied** {vid} to the players that see it, then the killer's PointsChanged with its experience (QCX's order, as in its fight capture), then **Flight** {kind 0, from the monster, to the killer} to the players that see the monster, however much experience it gave, none included (as the original server sends it, its source). It stops where it was.
- It lies there 5 s (`Config::corpse_time`), then the players that see it get **EntityRemove** (QCX).
- When the last monster of its group is removed, the whole group comes back after the group's `respawn` time, each monster at a random point of its area with all its health, shown to the players near (QCX). Stones come back the same way.
- **A pack** comes back at a new random point of its group's area, its leader there and the others within 5 m of it, as when the server started (openm2: QCX draws every monster's point again). Each pack of a group comes back on its own.
- What it leaves on the ground follows its death (A monster's drops).

## Stones
A stone with `calls` ([`content.md`](content.md), "Monster") calls packs as it loses health:
- **When:** its health counts in tenths, full health the tenth 10. A hit that leaves it alive in a lower tenth than the one it last called at calls one of its packs, at random (openm2: QCX calls all of a stone's groups each time); once a hit, however many tenths it took (QCX). So the first hit calls, and a stone calls at most 10 times.
- **Where:** the pack's leader at a random open point within **15 m** of the stone (QCX puts each monster at its own random point within 15 m), the others within 5 m of the leader, as any pack ("Entities and vids"). They take new vids, are shown to the players near, and fight the player who hit the stone at once (QCX).
- **A group of its own:** the pack fights and strolls as any pack, but never comes back: once its last monster is removed, its monsters leave the world and their vids are free again (openm2).
- **The stone's death:** the monsters it called that still live fall with it, EntityDied to the players that see them, giving no experience and dropping nothing (QCX); they lie as any corpse, then go.
- A stone comes back with its group (A monster's death) at full health, and calls again.

## Experience and levels
- **Who:** the player who landed the killing blow, alone (QCX).
- **How much:** the monster's `experience` × (100 % + 5 % for each level the monster is above the player, at most 180 %; − 5 % for each level below, at least 1 %), rounded down (openm2: QCX has a table of its own).
- **The levels:** what each level needs is the content's `levels.toml` ([`content.md`](content.md)), which ends one level below the highest; without it, 200 × level² + 100 × level, up to level 99 (`Config::max_level`) (openm2).
- **A level-up:** the level goes up by one for each level the experience fills; +3 status points and +1 skill point each (openm2: QCX gives the status points at quarters of a level, without a level-up); the maxima are worked out again and health and mana filled (openm2: QCX does neither). The player gets PointsChanged; it and the players that see it get **EntityEffect** of a level gained (kind 2, as the original server tells them), then its **EntityInfo** with the new level (QCX).
- At the highest level no experience is gathered (openm2: QCX takes it and loses it).

## Dying
- A player whose health reaches 0 stops where it is; it and the players that see it get **EntityDied** {its vid} (QCX). It keeps its experience (QCX). The monsters fighting it give it up at their next step.
- The dead do not move or hit: their CharacterMove and Hit are ignored (openm2). The client shows the restart window.
- **Restarting:** **Restart** {town}, only while dead and at once (QCX's chat commands `/restart_here` and `/restart_town`; openm2 makes it a message):
  - it comes back where it fell, or at its empire's start (openm2: QCX reads the map's town position);
  - with 50 health and mana (QCX), at most its maxima (`Config::restart_points`);
  - the monsters still fighting it give it up;
  - it gets its own **EntityRemove**, **EntitySpawn** (state 0), **EntityInfo**, then what comes into and goes out of its view, then PointsChanged; the players that still see it get its EntityRemove, EntitySpawn and EntityInfo (and EntityMount and EntitySkills, as a warp sends them below), those near its new place its spawn (QCX's order, without the chat line and the second game phase QCX sends first: the client closes its restart window when its character's spawn says it lives).
- A character that leaves the game dead is saved dead and enters dead, with the restart window (openm2: QCX fills its health at login).

## Regeneration
- Every **3 s** (`Config::regen_every`, from entering or restarting), a living player regains 15 + 5 % of its maximum health and mana standing, 15 + 1 % walking, up to the maxima, and gets PointsChanged when anything changed (QCX).

## Items
Where items are and what the client is told ([`inventory.md`](../protocol/openm2/inventory.md)); item numbers, sizes, flags and values are the content's, in the client's numbering ([`content.md`](content.md), "Items").

- **The bag** (QCX): window 1, places 0 to 89 in two pages of 5 × 9, numbered page by page, row by row, left to right. An item of size *s* (1 to 3; the client draws none taller) covers its place and the *s* − 1 below it, all on one page. The **first free place** for an item is the first, in that order, where all its places are free (QCX).
- **Worn items** (QCX) are in window 1 too, at 90 + their position: body 0, head 1, shoes 2, bracelet 3, weapon 4, necklace 5, earrings 6, unique items 7 and 8, arrows 9, shield 10. An item's position comes from the first of its wear flags in the order head (2), shoes (4), bracelet (8), weapon (16), necklace (32), earrings (64), body (1), shield (256) (QCX), then unique (128) and arrows (512) (openm2: QCX breaks on them and loses shields); a unique item takes place 8 when 7 is taken and 8 free. Costumes come later.
- **Who may use an item** (QCX): its anti flags refuse a sex (female 1, male 2: races 1, 3, 4 and 6 are female) or a class (warrior 4, ninja 8, sura 16, shaman 32, the race modulo 4), and its level limit (limit kind 1) a lower level.
- **Stacks** (openm2): an item with the stackable flag (4) holds up to 200 in one place; anything else one.
- **Sockets**: every item has three numbers, kept when it is moved, worn, dropped and picked up, and saved with it; items stack only with the same sockets, and new items (a shop's, a drop's, `/item`) have none. A fishing pole keeps its points and bait in them ("Fishing").
- **Entering** (QCX's order): after selection, the loading phase, MainCharacter and Points, a **QuickSlotSet** for each quickslot that holds something; after the game phase and the player's own EntitySpawn and EntityInfo, an **ItemSet** {window 1, place, number, count, sockets, the rest 0, no bonuses} for each item of the bag, then each worn one, by place. A character's stored items that cannot lie where they were stored go to the first free place, and those with no place at all are left out with an event in the log (openm2).
- **Gear** (QCX): what the worn items give, worked out from them whenever needed:
  - others see the worn armour and weapon: EntityInfo's and EntityUpdate's parts are {armour's number, weapon's number, 0, 0}, and the character list's body part the armour's number;
  - defence + value 1 + 2 × value 5 of each worn armour (item type 2);
  - attack speed and movement speed × (100 + the sum of the worn items' applies 7 and 8) / 100, from 1 to 255 (openm2: QCX counts movement only on armour); a walk's duration uses that movement speed;
  - a worn weapon (item type 1) replaces the fists (Damage).
- **UseItem** {window 1, place}, in game and alive (openm2: QCX lets the dead), else ignored (outside the game the connection closes):
  - a worn item comes off to the first free place; with no room it stays on and the player hears "Your bag is full." (QCX, in our words);
  - an item that is worn goes on at its position if the player may use it, else the player hears why: "Your character cannot wear this." (its class or sex) or "You need level N to wear this." (openm2: QCX says nothing); what was worn there goes back to the used item's place if it fits there, else to the first free place (QCX puts it at both and keeps a ghost); with no room nothing changes and the player hears why;
  - a **potion** (item type 3, subtype 0, or 11 for one that acts at once) gives back value 0 health and value 1 mana, and of subtype 11 also values 3 and 4 percent of the most health and mana, up to the maxima, and one is used up; one that would give nothing back is kept (openm2: QCX has no potions);
  - a **skill book** (type 17), a **book that forgets** (type 22), a **soul stone** or an **item that helps** (`training.toml`) trains or forgets skills ("Training" below);
  - an **upgrade scroll** (`upgrades.toml`) says where it goes: `Put the scroll onto the item you want to upgrade.` ("Upgrades" below);
  - **bait**, an item that **opens**, the **fire's** item, an item that **raises** a point for a while, a **hiding** item and a **cure** do what "Fishing" says;
  - anything else does nothing; other refusals are silent (QCX).
  - Gear changes answer **EntityUpdate** {vid, parts, movement speed, attack speed, 0, …} to the player and the players that see it, then PointsChanged, then the ItemSets: the old place emptied, the new one, the item that went back (QCX's order). A potion answers ItemSet then PointsChanged, and TargetHealth to those looking at the player; then, to the player and the players that see it, **EntityEffect** for the health it gave back (kind 0) and for the mana (kind 1), as the original server sends them.
- **MoveItem** {from window and place, to window and place, count}, in game and alive: the whole stack moves (QCX: the count is not read). Into the bag only where all its places are free, its own not counting (openm2: QCX counts them, so a tall item cannot move one row down); onto the body only to an empty position that suits it and that the player may wear (QCX; one it may not wear gets the same line as UseItem, openm2); never from one worn place to another; a stackable item onto the same item tops that stack up to 200 and the rest stays (openm2: QCX refuses). Answer: EntityUpdate and PointsChanged when the gear changed, then ItemSet for the old place and the new one (QCX); refusals are silent (QCX).
- **Quickslots:** 32, 0 to 31, four pages of eight as the original's taskbar has them (QCX had eight, 0 to 7). **SetQuickSlot** {index, kind, position} holds an item of the bag (kind 1, place below 90) or a skill (kind 2) and is echoed by **QuickSlotSet**; **ClearQuickSlot** by **QuickSlotCleared**; **SwapQuickSlots** by **QuickSlotsSwapped**. Anything else is ignored (openm2: QCX stores any kind and position). Quickslots do not follow moved items (QCX); the dead may change them. They are saved with the character.
- **Gold** is a checked number up to `Config::max_gold`, never below 0 (openm2).
- Items and quickslots are saved with the character, in its transaction ([`storage.md`](storage.md)).

## The ground
- **Ground items** are entities with vids, seen by the rules of View from where they lie: coming into view sends **GroundItemAdd** {vid, number (1 for gold), where} then **ItemOwnership** {vid, the owner's name, empty when anyone may take it}; going out of view, being picked up or vanishing sends **GroundItemRemove** {vid} (QCX).
- **DropItem** {window, place, count} and **DropGold** {amount}, in game and alive (openm2: QCX lets the dead):
  - DropGold: that much out of the purse (1 to what it holds), then PointsChanged, and gold on the ground (QCX);
  - DropItem: some or all of a bag item: window 1, a place below 90 (openm2: QCX drops worn items too), a count from 1 to the stack (QCX drops 0-count items); the place's ItemSet with what is left, or emptied (QCX);
  - it lands where the player is, with no owner (QCX), and the players that see the spot see it;
  - at most 10 drops a player in any 10 s (`Config::drop_burst`, `drop_window`), and nothing past 20,000 items on the ground (`Config::max_ground_items`) (openm2); refusals are silent;
  - the player is **saved at once** (openm2), so a dropped item has left its storage before anyone can pick it up and be saved with it: an item is never in two characters' storage, even if the server stops between two saves.
- **PickUp** {vid}, in game and alive: an item the player sees, within 5 m of where it is (`Config::pick_up_reach`; openm2: QCX checks no distance), and not someone else's while it is owned (QCX; gold too, openm2):
  - gold goes to the purse, up to `Config::max_gold` (openm2), then PointsChanged (QCX);
  - an item goes onto the bag's stacks of it first, then to the first free place (openm2: QCX never merges), with an ItemSet for each place changed and "You picked up *name* (*count*)." in the chat (QCX, in our words); all of it or nothing;
  - then it leaves the ground at once: GroundItemRemove to everyone who sees it (openm2: QCX waits for its next map update, and a second pick-up before then gets it twice);
  - someone else's item, a full purse or a full bag leave it there with a line in the chat ("That is not yours yet.", "You cannot carry more gold.", "Your bag is full."); anything else is ignored.
- **Owners and time** (openm2: QCX keeps both forever): a monster's drop belongs to its killer for 30 s (`Config::owner_time`), then the players that see it get ItemOwnership with no name; every item vanishes 5 min after it lands (`Config::ground_time`). Items stay on the ground when the players who saw them leave.

## A monster's drops
- When a monster dies, after the killer's experience (openm2: QCX drops no gold and rolls drops from its own tables):
  - gold: the operator's `[[gold]]` for it by its chance, a random amount from its least to its most ([`content.md`](content.md), "Gold"); without one, from its own `gold` range whenever the most is above 0;
  - each of its drops in `drops.toml` by its chance, with its count ([`content.md`](content.md), "Drops");
  - each where it died, up to 50 cm off each way so they do not lie on one point (openm2: QCX puts them on the point), owned by the killer by its name (QCX).

## Shops
- **Goods** come from `shops.toml` ([`content.md`](content.md), "Shops"): one shop per NPC number, every NPC of that number has it (QCX). Its places are 5 wide and 8 high; each good takes the first free place, row by row from the top left, with room for its height (the item's size), and covers the places under it (QCX). A good's price is for the whole stack: written in `shops.toml`, else the item's buy price × the count (openm2: QCX takes the buy price once, whatever the count).
- **Talk** {vid}, in game and alive: an NPC the player sees, within **5 m** (`Config::talk_reach`; openm2: QCX checks neither and closes the connection for an entity it cannot find). An NPC with goods opens its shop: **ShopOpened** {the NPC's vid, its goods by place: place, number, price, count; empty places left out} (openm2: QCX sends 0 for the vid). It replaces any shop open before. An NPC without goods, or anything else, does nothing.
- **The open shop** stays the player's until ShopClose, another Talk that opens one, or a purchase or sale when its NPC is gone or more than **12 m** away (`Config::shop_reach`), which forgets it (openm2: QCX keeps it however far the player walks). The server sends no close of its own (QCX); the client closes its window when the NPC leaves its view.
- **ShopBuy** {place}, in game and alive, with a shop open: the good at that place, the whole stack (QCX):
  - less gold than its price: **ShopRefused** {1}, nothing else (QCX);
  - no room in the bag (onto stacks of the item first, then the first free place): **ShopRefused** {2}, nothing taken (openm2: QCX takes the gold anyway);
  - otherwise the gold goes, the item goes into the bag, and PointsChanged and then an ItemSet for each place it changed follow (QCX's order);
  - an empty place, or no shop open, is ignored (openm2: QCX closes the connection for an empty place).
- **ShopSell** {place, count}, in game and alive, with a shop open: `count` (1 to the stack's) of the item at a place of the bag (0 to 89; worn items cannot be sold), for the item's sell value each: its sell price, but never more than its buy price when it has one, so buying and selling an item back never makes gold (openm2: QCX sells the whole stack and pays the sell price once; the client's tables sell some items for more than they cost). ItemSet for the place (what is left, or empty), then PointsChanged (QCX's order). Gold past `Config::max_gold` is refused with "You cannot carry more gold." in the chat (openm2); anything else is ignored.
- **ShopClose**: the shop is forgotten (QCX).
- Requests are handled in the order they come, so buys and sells sent just before a ShopClose still count (QCX, as its shop capture shows).
- Outside the game, any of these closes the connection, as every game message does.

## Skills
- **The skills** come from `skills.toml` ([`content.md`](content.md), "Skills"), which the import takes from the client's skill table. A class's job is its number modulo 4; each job has two **skill groups** of six skills: group 1 from 30 × job + 1, group 2 fifteen further on (warrior 1 to 6 and 16 to 21, ninja 31 to 36 and 46 to 51, sura 61 to 66 and 76 to 81, shaman 91 to 96 and 106 to 111) (QCX). Passive, horse and guild skills are not used yet.
- **The skill group** is 0 until the player chooses one, once, from level 5 (`Config::skill_group_level`); it gets **SkillGroup** {group} and the event (openm2: QCX sets it with its GM command `/setjob`, from level 5; the original's way is a quest at a teacher, by what is known). The group is in MainCharacter and the character list, and kept with the character.
  - **At a teacher** (`teachers.toml`, [`content.md`](content.md), "Teachers"): Talk to a teacher within `talk_reach` sends **TeacherOpened** {vid, job, group, level: `skill_group_level`} and forgets any shop open. **LearnSkillGroup** {vid} is checked from scratch: in game (else the connection closes), alive, the NPC seen, a teacher, within `shop_reach` (12 m, the reach a conversation stays open); then `This teacher teaches another class.` for another job, `You have chosen your skills already.` with a group, `You can choose your skills from level N.` below the level; else the group, SkillGroup and `You have chosen your skills: spend skill points on them.` Anything else is ignored (openm2).
  - **With `/skillgroup 1`** or **`2`**: every player's in a world without teachers, a game master's in one with them (`Choose skill group 1 or 2: /skillgroup 1` for another argument, `You have chosen your skill group already.`, `You can choose a skill group from level N.`).
  - **The reminder** (openm2): while the character has no group, the line `You can choose your skills now: talk to a teacher of your class.` (`/skillgroup 1 or 2` without teachers) after entering from that level on, and after the level-up or `/level` that reaches it. RaiseSkill without a group says `Choose a skill group first:` and the same way.
- **Skills** {the skills learned: number, level, grade, `next_read` ("Training")} goes out after entering, after the bag's ItemSets (QCX), and after every point spent; skills not learned are left out (openm2: QCX sent all 255). Levels 1 to 19 are normal, 20 to 29 master, 30 to 39 grand master, 40 perfect master; the grade follows the level (QCX).
- **Skill points** come with level-ups (Experience and levels). **RaiseSkill** {skill} spends one on a skill, open to every player (openm2: QCX's chat command `/skillup`, for GMs only): the skill must be one of the player's group that the content has, at a character level of at least the skill's `level`, below level 20 (points take a skill to M1; books and soul stones take it further, "Training"). At level 17 a point makes the skill a master at once one time in 4, at 18 one time in 3 (QCX). The player gets PointsChanged, then Skills (QCX's order). Refusals get a line in the chat: no group yet, not a skill it can learn, its level too low, a master's skill, no points left.
- **A skill's power** at its level, the `k` of its formulas: level × 0.025, so 0.5 at M1 and 1 at a perfect master (`Config::skill_power`; openm2: QCX evaluates no formula). Formulas are worked out in whole thousandths ([`content.md`](content.md), "Formulas"); `atk` is the player's attack against the target (Damage, steps 1 to 3, with its own roll), `mwep` the weapon's roll (openm2: magic weapons' own values are not read yet), `ar` its hit rating / 100, `odef` the target's defence, `chain` how many monsters the use hit before this one, `lv`, `str`, `dex`, `con`, `iq`, `maxhp` and `maxsp` the player's own.
- **Using a skill:** the client sends the skill's motion as a move (bit 7 set in the move's kind, relayed to the players around as EntityMove, as any move), then **UseSkill** {skill, target} ([`skills.md`](../protocol/openm2/skills.md)). In game and alive, for a learned skill of the player's group (openm2: QCX only logs it):
  - its cooldown must be over, else "The skill is ready again in N s." in the chat (openm2). The server keeps the cooldown itself and never takes the client's word for it; `play` shows the same cooldown on the skill's icons and does not send an early use, working it out from the client's skill table with the same skill power ([`ui.md`](../ui.md), "Skills"), but a client that sends one anyway is refused here;
  - its mana cost (`sp_cost`, rounded down) must be paid, else "You do not have enough mana." in the chat;
  - a skill that stays on (flag `toggle`) and is on goes off instead, at once and for nothing, riding too ("Skill effects");
  - a skill used on others names a player it may go on, else the user ("Skill effects");
  - the mana goes, its **cooldown** (`cooldown`, in seconds, shorter by the user's casting speed) starts, and PointsChanged follows;
  - an **attack skill** (flag `attack`) opens a window of 3 s (`Config::skill_window`) for its hits;
  - a skill **used on oneself** (flag `selfonly`) raises its points (`point` by `amount` for `duration` seconds, and `point2` by `amount2` for `duration2`): attack speed and movement speed in percent (`att_speed`, `mov_speed`), the attack grade (`att_grade`), defence (`def_grade`), and those of "Skill effects". An attack skill raises its points but health on the user too (Dash's movement speed). Using it again replaces what it raised. The player and the players that see it get its EntityUpdate (speeds, `affects`), then the player its PointsChanged and **Affects**; the same when the raised points end;
  - an attack skill's hits name their targets; UseSkill's target counts for a skill used on others.
- **A skill's hits:** Hit {vid, skill} while its window lasts: on a monster or stone the player sees and that lives, within the larger of 5 m (`Config::attack_reach`), the skill's `target_range` and its `splash_range`; on at most `max_hit` monsters per use (at least one), at most 5 times each (`Config::skill_hits`), no sooner than 150 ms after the player's last hit on that monster (`Config::hit_gap`) (openm2: QCX takes every skill's hit for a normal one). The damage is the skill's negative `amount`, less the monster's defence (Damage, steps 4 and 5) unless the skill's kind is `magic` or it has the flag `compute_magic_damage` (then at least 1). Then as any hit: Damage, TargetHealth, the monster's answer or death. A hit outside a window, of another skill, or past these limits is ignored.
- **Dying** ends the skill in use, what skills raised and the skills that stay on; the player gets an empty Affects.

## Skill effects
What skills do besides their damage and the speeds and grades they raise (openm2's reading of the skill table's words, and openm2's own numbers, `Config`'s, where neither QCX nor the client says).
- **Affects** {skill, point, amount, seconds} lists what skills raise on the player for now, the user's own and others' on it: the whole list whenever it changes (a use, an end, a toggle), empty when the last ends ([`skills.md`](../protocol/openm2/skills.md), "What skills raise"). The points: 1 attack speed, 2 movement speed, 3 attack grade, 4 defence, 5 resistance, 6 reflection, 7 critical hits, 8 dodging, 9 health from hits, 10 mana shield, 11 casting speed, 12 hidden; 0 for a skill that is on and raises nothing. `seconds` is what is left, rounded up, 0 while a skill that stays on is on. Raised points are not saved: a logout ends them.
- **Skills used on others:** a skill that neither attacks nor is `selfonly` goes on UseSkill's `target` when that names a living player in game the user sees, within the skill's `target_range` (else `Config::attack_reach`) and a metre more for what moved meanwhile; on the user without a target or with its own vid. A monster or an NPC gets `This skill is used on players.`, a player too far `Too far away.`, and neither costs anything; anyone else is ignored. What it raises goes on the target, worked out with the user's points; the target gets its EntityUpdate, points and Affects. A positive `hp` heals the target up to its maximum: Damage {target, flags 64, the health given} to the target and the user, its points, its health to the players looking at it. `remove_bad_affect` would end the target's states, which players don't have.
- **More points** skills raise, from any of the user's skills and others' on it:
  - `resist_normal`: a monster's bite takes that percent less, at most 90 (at least 1 when it took any);
  - `manashield`: after resistance, half of a bite (`Config::manashield_share`, 50) is taken from mana instead of health, at the point's value in percent of mana per health point spared (rounded up), as far as the mana pays;
  - `dodge`: that percent chance a shot is dodged, added to the worn items' (below, "Misses");
  - `reflect_melee`: after the bite, that percent of the health it took goes back to a monster that doesn't shoot, as the player's hit (Damage, the monster's answer or death);
  - `critical`: that percent chance a player's hit or a skill's does double (Damage flags 33);
  - `hit_hp_recover`: that percent of the damage a player's hit does comes back as its health;
  - `casting_speed`: the cooldowns of skills used meanwhile are that percent shorter, at most 90.
- **Misses** (as the original server has them, its source): a monster's melee bite may be blocked, with the chance the player's worn items' applies of kind 27 (`APPLY_BLOCK`) add up to; a shot (a monster that shoots, "Monsters fighting back") may be dodged, with the chance their applies of kind 28 (`APPLY_DODGE`) and what skills raise of `dodge` add up to; each at most 90 % (openm2's cap). A blocked bite sends Damage {the player, flags 8, 0}, a dodged one flags 4, to the player; nothing is taken and nothing reflected. Players' hits on monsters never miss: monsters have neither chance.
- **Skills on players, for others to see**: whenever a player's Affects goes out and the skills on it changed since the players that see it were told (those its raised points come from and those it has on, by number), it and the players that see it get **EntitySkills** {vid, skills}, empty when the last ends; a player who comes to see it gets it after its spawn and info when skills are on it ([`entities.md`](../protocol/openm2/entities.md), "Skills on players").
- **Hiding** (a `selfonly` skill that raises nothing and lasts, Stealth): the user is hidden for its duration (point 12, `affects` bit 4 for the players that see it). Aggressive monsters don't notice it ("Monsters fighting back"), those fighting it give it up at their next step and don't turn on it after another. It ends when the user hits or uses a skill.
- **States on monsters**, from an attack skill's hits that land: each of its flags `attack_poison`, `attack_fire_cont`, `attack_slow` and `attack_stun` gives its state with its percent chance times the skill's power (`k`: half at M1), rolled with the world's random numbers. A state lasts from the hit, and a hit that gives it again starts it again. The players that see the monster get its EntityUpdate (`affects`: bit 0 poisoned, 1 burning, 2 slowed, 3 stunned; its movement speed) when a state comes or goes, and in its spawn.

  | State | Chance at P | Lasts | What |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | poisoned | 60 % | 10 s | 1 % of its maximum health a second (Damage flags 2) |
  | burning | 60 % | 6 s | 2 % a second (Damage flags 128) |
  | slowed | 60 % | 6 s | 30 % less movement speed |
  | stunned | 40 % | 2 s | it stops (EntityMove kind 0), and neither moves nor bites until it ends |

  - Poison and fire take their health every second from the hit (at least 1) as hits of the player who gave them, to that player (Damage) and the players looking at the monster (TargetHealth): the monster turns on it, and a kill is its. A state ends early when its giver leaves the game or no longer sees the monster, and when the monster dies.
- **How a skill's hit lands:** `crush` pushes the monster away from the user, 3 m (`Config::crush_reach`), else 1.5 m or 75 cm when a wall is in the way, else not at all, over 200 ms (an EntityMove kind 1 to where it ends, the monster still facing the user); a monster that doesn't move stays. `penetrate`: the monster's defence counts for nothing (Damage flags 17). `ignore_target_rating`: the monster's hit rating counts as 0.
- **Skills that stay on** (flag `toggle`): a use turns the skill on, with its cooldown and mana as any use; the next use turns it off, at once and for nothing. While on, what it raises lasts without a duration, and every 5 s (`Config::toggle_every`) it costs its `duration_sp_cost`, worked out at its level; when the mana doesn't pay, it ends, with `Not enough mana: Enchanted Blade ends.` An attack skill that stays on (Flame Spirit) hits every 2 s (`Config::spirit_every`): the user's target when it is a monster within the skill's `target_range` (else `Config::attack_reach`), else the nearest monster fighting the user within it; its damage as a skill hit's, and the hit lands as above. It doesn't hit while its user rides, as a job's skills are used on foot ("Mounts"); its mana is still taken.

## Training
Past a master's, skills train with items (openm2: QCX has none of it, and the original's numbers are not known). The numbers are `training.toml`'s ([`content.md`](content.md), "Training"), with openm2's own by default.
- **The wall clock:** waits are kept in Unix seconds, so that they outlive a logout and a restart: the core's Unix time is the epoch the edge passed at its start (`Server::started_at`, the system's clock in `m2-server`) plus the seconds since. Each learned skill keeps `next_read`, when it may train again (0 for any time), and the character `read_bonus`, the percent its next training is likelier. Both are saved with the character ([`storage.md`](storage.md)).
- **Who may train a skill:** a skill of the character's group, learned, at the levels the item trains, its wait over. Else a line and nothing used: `Choose a skill group first:` and the way (no group), `This book is not about one of your skills.` (a stone's: `This is not one of your skills.`), `Only a master learns from a book: raise the skill to M1 first.`, `A book teaches a grand master nothing: train the skill with a soul stone.`, `Only a grand master's skill trains with a soul stone.`, `This skill is perfect already.`, `You can read about this skill again in 42 min.` (a stone's: `You can train this skill again in …`; waits under a minute in seconds, else whole minutes rounded up, `1 h`, `1 h 5 min`).
- **Training:** one item is used (ItemSet with what is left); the chance plus `read_bonus`, at most 100, is rolled with the world's random numbers, and `read_bonus` goes back to 0. The skill rises a level, or doesn't; either way it waits the practice's `wait`. The line says which (`The book taught you: Wide Swing is M3 now.`, `The book taught you nothing this time.`, `The soul stone worked: …`, `The soul stone did nothing this time.`), then Skills, and the event `SkillTrained`.
- **A skill book** (item type 17, its skill in value 0): trains its skill from M1 to M10, and M10 to G1, with `[books]`' chance and wait. A book of no skill the content has says `This book teaches no skill.`
- **A soul stone** (`[stones]`' items): UseItem answers **ChooseSkill** {its place, the group's skills from G1 to G10 whose wait is over}, nothing used; with none, `Only a grand master's skill trains with a soul stone.` or `Your skills need rest: the next is ready in 42 min.` **UseItemOnSkill** {place, skill} is checked from scratch, with nothing kept from ChooseSkill: in game (else the connection closes), alive, a place of the bag holding a soul stone; then the skill as above (a line for each refusal), and it trains with `[stones]`' chance and wait, G10 to P. Anything else is ignored.
- **Items that help** (`[[help]]`), used from the bag: `skip_wait` sets every skill's `next_read` to 0 (`Your skills are ready to train again.`, then Skills); `chance` becomes `read_bonus` (`Your next training is 25% more likely to work.`). One is used only when it does something: no skill waiting (`None of your skills is waiting.`) or a bonus held already (`You are concentrated already.`) keeps it.
- **A book that forgets** (item type 22): every skill of the character's job goes (30 × job + 1 to 30 × job + 30), and its group, and the cooldowns of those skills; the quickslots that held them are cleared (QuickSlotCleared each); its skill points become one a level-up from level 1 (`level − 1`), so what early masters and books gave is gone too. SkillGroup 0, PointsChanged, Skills, the event `SkillsForgotten`, then `You have forgotten your skills. Choose again:` and the way to choose. Nothing to forget (no group, no skill of the job): `You have nothing to forget.` and nothing used. What its skills raised for a while runs out on its own.

## Mounts
openm2's own ([`../protocol/openm2/mounts.md`](../protocol/openm2/mounts.md); the content in [`content.md`](content.md), "Mounts"). In a world with `mounts.toml`, a character has one mount, at a level from 0 (none) to the last; its level, stamina and horseback skill points are saved with it. It always enters on foot, its mount away; loading clamps the level to the last and the stamina to the level's most.
- **The call** (the `summon` item, never used up): with no mount, `You have no mount.`; riding, the rider gets off and the mount goes away; a mount in the world goes away; else it comes 1.5 m beside its rider (to its left, else another of eight ways, on an open square a straight walk reaches), an entity of kind 8 that looks like its grade's `look`, walks at its look's run speed and its level's `move_speed`, cannot be fought or talked to, and follows its rider. MountState after each.
- **Getting on** (RideMount true), each change at least `Config::ride_gap` (1 s) after the last (sooner is ignored): alive, a mount, not riding, no shop open (`Close the shop first.`), stamina above 0 and, after tiring out, back to `Config::mount_rested` (20 %) of its most (`Your mount is too tired: let it rest.`), and the mount within `Config::mount_reach` (5 m; `Your mount is too far away.`) or away with its call in the bag (`Call your mount first.` without). The mount leaves the world; EntityMount to the rider and its watchers, then the rider's EntityUpdate and points, RunSpeed and MountState. **Getting off:** EntityMount on foot, EntityUpdate, points, RunSpeed; the mount appears beside the rider and follows; MountState.
- **Speeds:** riding, the run speed is the look's (`run_speed`, else `Config::run_speed`) and the movement speed the level's `move_speed`: gear and skills do not count (openm2's choice). Walks are checked and timed with them; a walk under way when they change keeps its timing.
- **Following:** every `Config::mount_follow_every` (300 ms), a called mount whose rider's walk ends more than `Config::mount_follow_gap` (3 m) from where its own does walks to that far short of it, straight or along the first leg of a path round the walls (`walk.md`); a mount on its way there walks on. Farther than `Config::mount_leash` (30 m) from its rider, or with no way there, it appears beside its rider again (EntityRemove, then EntitySpawn). It goes when its rider leaves the game; nothing fights it and monsters do not turn on it.
- **Stamina,** every second: a mount ridden while its rider walks loses `drain`; any other gets `rest` back up to its level's most. One worn out to 0 puts its rider off (`Your mount is worn out: let it rest.`), stands beside it and is tired out. Each change sends MountState. **Food** (a `[[feed]]` item, used from the bag): one is eaten for its stamina, up to the most; at the most it is kept (`Your mount is not hungry.`).
- **Stables:** Talk to a `[[stable]]` NPC sends StableOpened (teachers first, then stables, then shops), forgetting any shop open. TrainMount, within `Config::shop_reach`, alive and on foot (`Get off your mount first.`): the next level (`Your mount cannot be trained any further.` past the last), its `rider_level` (`You need level N to train your mount.`), its gold (`You do not have enough gold.`) and items (`You need N × NAME.`), and for the first the room for the call (`Your bag is full.`). The costs are taken, the level raised, the stamina filled and the level's skill points given; the first gives the call. A mount in the world whose look changed comes again in its new one. PointsChanged, the ItemSets that changed, MountState, StableOpened, `Your mount is now level N.` (`You have a mount now: call it with its item.` for the first) and the event.
- **Fighting:** a rider on a grade without `fights` does not hit (its Hits are ignored). Dying puts a rider off before EntityDied, its mount beside it; a restart is on foot.
- **Horseback skills** (`job = "horse"`): RaiseSkill raises them with the mount's skill points, as group skills with the character's (`You need a mount for this skill.`, `Your mount has no skill points left.`), and MountState follows Skills. UseSkill takes them only riding a grade with `skills` (`Ride your mount to use this skill.`, `Your mount cannot use this skill.`) and job skills only on foot (`Get off your mount to use this skill.`). One whose flags hold `charge` lets its rider's next skill move go as far as its `target_range` (and the 2 m every move has) within `Config::skill_window`; once (`session.md`, "Moving").

## Upgrades
openm2's own ([`../protocol/openm2/upgrades.md`](../protocol/openm2/upgrades.md); the content in [`content.md`](content.md), "Upgrades"). An item of the bag upgrades to its `upgrade` item by its `upgrade_set`, at a smith NPC or with a scroll, when `upgrades.toml` has that set. An upgrade changes the number in its place and keeps its count and sockets; the character's save stores it. A fisher betters a full fishing pole through the same messages ("Fishing").
- **At a smith:** Talk to a `[[smith]]` NPC answers `Drag an item from your bag onto the smith to upgrade it.` (teachers first, then stables, then smiths, then shops), forgetting any shop open. **GiveItem** {vid, place}, in game, alive, the NPC seen within `Config::talk_reach`: a smith answers **UpgradeOffer**, another NPC `Only a smith upgrades items.`; anything else is ignored (outside the game the connection closes).
- **With a scroll:** **UseItemOnItem** {place, onto}, in game and alive, both of the bag and not the same: a `[[scroll]]` at `place` answers UpgradeOffer for the item at `onto`; any other item does nothing. A scroll whose `up_to` the next item would pass is refused (`This scroll upgrades items only up to +N.`, the next item's level being how many upgrades lead to it).
- **The offer** (both ways): the item must be in the bag (a worn one: `Take the item off first.`), one of it (`Upgrade one item at a time.`), with an `upgrade` and its set (`This item cannot be upgraded.`, or `This item cannot be upgraded any further.` for the last of a line), and the next item must fit its place (`There is no room in your bag for the upgraded item.`). UpgradeOffer {smith or scroll, place, the item, the next, the set's gold and items, the chance, what a failure does}: the set's chance at a smith; with a scroll the set's plus its `chance`, at most 100, or 100 when `sure`. A smith's or scroll's `on_fail` `down` is sent as `kept` when nothing upgrades to the item or what does would not fit its place.
- **Upgrade** {smith or scroll, place, vnum}, in game: checked again from scratch (no state is kept between the offer and it): the smith seen within `Config::shop_reach` and alive, or the scroll still at its place; the item `vnum` still at the place (else ignored, as with both or neither of smith and scroll); the offer's rules; the gold (`You do not have enough gold.`) and the items (`You need N × NAME.`), which must not include the item upgraded itself (`You need more NAME than the one you upgrade.`). Then the gold and the items are taken, and one scroll; the chance is rolled with the world's random numbers (`below(100) < chance`); the item becomes the next, or a failure leaves the place empty (`destroyed`), puts the item one level down in it (`down`) or keeps it (`kept`). PointsChanged, an ItemSet for each place that changed, the event and a line: `The upgrade worked: NAME.`, `The upgrade failed, and the NAME broke.`, `The upgrade failed: the NAME is now NAME.` or `The upgrade failed: the NAME is unchanged.`

## Warps
As the original's (the content in [`content.md`](content.md), "Monster"). A warp (kind `warp`) whose `to` is a point of the world takes players there; a goto (kind `goto`) to its `to` on the map it stands on, from the map's corner. In a world with maps, one leading to no map, and a goto standing on none, take nobody. They are entities of kind 3 and 9: players see them (`play` draws nothing for them), nothing fights them.
- **Every half second** (the original's), each warp takes each living player in the game within 3 m of it (where its walk has it then), one warp a player; not one with a shop open (the original takes nobody busy with a trade). Nobody asks: walking in is enough.
- **Taken:** the player stops where its walk had it, the monsters fighting it give it up, and it is put at the place at once: it gets its own **EntityRemove**, **EntitySpawn** and **EntityInfo** (and **EntityMount** while it rides), then what comes into and goes out of its view there, on whatever map; the players that saw it and still see it get its EntityRemove, EntitySpawn, EntityInfo, EntityMount and EntitySkills, the others its EntityRemove; aggressive monsters near its new place notice it; and the event. Its mount, if one follows it, comes beside it at its next step (it is past the leash). A move the client sent from where it was is checked from the new place and refused (`MoveRefused`, the place).
- The place is not checked against the walk masks (the original's isn't either): the content's warps lead to open ground.

## Fishing
As the original's, checked by the server where the original trusts its client ([`../protocol/openm2/fishing.md`](../protocol/openm2/fishing.md); the content in [`content.md`](content.md), "Fishing"). Without `fishing.toml` no map has fish: every cast is refused.
- **The pole:** an item of type 13 worn as the weapon. Its item table's values are its level (0), the 1 in N chance of a point (1), the most points (2), the percent chance a fisher betters it (3) and the pole a failure gives (4); its `upgrade` is the next pole. Its sockets hold its points (0) and the bait on it (2, the bait's item number).
- **Bait** (type 3, subtype 6; value 0 its strength): UseItem with a pole worn and no line out puts it on the pole and uses one up: `You put a NAME on the hook.`; bait already on it comes back into the bag (`…; the NAME went back into your bag.`, refused when there is no room); the same bait again is refused, the item kept (`The NAME is already on the hook.`). Without a pole: `Hold a fishing pole to put bait on it.`; with a line out: `Not while your line is out.`
- **Cast** {angle}, in game: refused, with a line, on a mount (`Get off your mount to fish.`), walking (`Stand still to fish.`), without a pole (`Hold a fishing pole to fish.`) or bait (`Put bait on the hook first.`), on a map without a `[[water]]` (`Nothing bites here.`), or when the float, `reach` out the way `angle` says (0 south, 90 east), isn't on a square of the map's water mask (`There is no water there.`). The dead, and a player with a line out, are ignored. The player turns that way, a shop it has open is forgotten, the bite's time is rolled with the world's random numbers (a whole second from `bite`'s first to its last), and **Fishing** (cast) goes to it and the players that see it.
- **The bite:** at its time the tick rolls what bit from the water of the map under the float (its weights, the empty hook's first) and sends Fishing (bite). A bite not reeled in within `react` is lost: the bait comes off the pole, Fishing (lost), `The fish got away with the bait.` (or `Something took the bait.` for an empty hook) and the event.
- **ReelIn**, in game: before a bite, Fishing (reeled in) and the bait stays. After one:
  - the pole, still held, may gain a point: by a chance of 1 in its value 1, if it has a next pole and isn't full;
  - the bait comes off the pole;
  - the catch takes two rolls: the fish's timing at the seconds since the bite (in percent, `below(10000) < chance × 100`), then `below(difficulty) < level`, the level being the pole's value 0 plus the bait's strength (the bait as it was on the pole when cast);
  - the pole's ItemSet, Fishing (caught) or Fishing (lost), then a catch's ItemSet (onto its stacks, else the first free place; with a full bag on the ground at the fisher's feet, its own for `Config::owner_time`, `Your bag is full: the catch is on the ground.`) and `You caught a NAME!` (a fish, type 12) or `You pulled a NAME out of the water!`; else `Nothing was on the hook.` or `The fish got away.`; then the points: `Your fishing pole has N of its M points.` or `Your fishing pole is full of points: the Fisherman can better it.`; and the event.
- **What ends a line** as a reel-in before a bite shows it, the bait kept before a bite and taken off the pole (wherever it now is) after one: a CharacterMove that isn't a stop where the player stands (walks, attacks, skills' moves), mounting, the pole's place changing (taken off, moved, another weapon put on), dying, a warp and a restart. Leaving the game drops the line with the player.
- **Watchers:** a player that comes to see a fisher with a line out gets Fishing (cast), and Fishing (bite) after a bite, with its EntitySpawn.
- **No skills with a pole:** UseSkill is refused (`Not with a fishing pole in hand.`), but a skill that stays on still goes off. Attacks aren't refused (the original's aren't); they end the line.
- **The Fisherman:** a `[[fisher]]` NPC (a shop of its own opens as any shop; without one, Talk answers `Drag a fishing pole full of points from your bag onto the Fisherman to better it.`). GiveItem of a pole of the bag answers UpgradeOffer {the fisher, the place, the pole, its next, gold 0, no items, its value 3, `down` (`kept` when its value 4 is itself or none)}: refused with a line for anything but a pole (`The Fisherman betters only fishing poles.`), a pole without a next one (`This fishing pole cannot be bettered any further.`), one not full (`This fishing pole has N of its M points: catch more fish with it first.`) or no room. Upgrade checks it again ("Upgrades") and rolls: the next pole, else the pole of its value 4 (itself when it names none), with no points or bait either way: `The Fisherman made your pole a NAME.`, `The Fisherman failed: your pole is now a NAME.` or `The Fisherman failed: your NAME starts again with no points.`
- **Opening:** UseItem of an `[[open]]` item: refused, the item kept, when the bag wouldn't have room for one of what it may give (`There is no room in your bag for what may be inside.`); else one is used up, `below(1000000) / 10000` picks a gift by its chances or nothing, and `Inside the NAME: a NAME.` (`N × NAME`) or `There was nothing inside the NAME.`
- **The fire:** UseItem of the fire's item lights its NPC 1 m before the player, the way it faces, facing it: refused on a mount (`Get off your mount to light a fire.`), and where the square is blocked, water or not reached straight (`You cannot light a fire here.`), the item kept. One is used up; the player's last fire goes out; `You lit a fire: drop fish onto it to grill them.` The fire is an NPC entity for `seconds`, then EntityRemove. GiveItem of an item of the bag onto a fire within talk reach: a `[[grill]]` item becomes its `into`, the whole stack, in its place (`You grilled N × NAME: NAME.`, or `You grilled the NAME: NAME.`); anything else `Only fish go on the fire.`
- **Boosts** (type 3): an ability item (subtype 7) whose value 0 is strength (5), dexterity (6), attack speed (7) or movement speed (8) raises it by value 2 for value 1 seconds; a hiding item (subtype 13) hides the player for 300 s, as Stealth does (monsters leave it be; others don't see it); using the same item again starts its time again. One is used up; EntityUpdate, PointsChanged, Affects (the skills' alone) and **ItemBoosts** (the items'). Strength and dexterity count in hits, bites and the points shown. Death ends them with the skills'. A cure (subtype 12) is refused, the item kept, while the player has no bad state (none are given to players yet): `There is nothing to cure.`
- **Not yet:** fish lengths, the fishing marble and a premium's better catches, the fishing skill on a quickslot.
