# Rendering

How openm2 draws the client's GR2 models (posing, skinning, textures, lighting, the camera), its maps (terrain, water, buildings, trees, the sky) and the interface over them. It draws them from an asset folder: the client's files converted by `openm2 assets import` ([`assets.md`](assets.md)), which carry over what is said of the client's files below. Why these libraries: [`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md#design-choices). The file formats are in `formats/gr2.md`, `formats/dds.md`, `formats/tga.md` and `formats/map.md`.

**Code:** `crates/m2-render` (`pose.rs`, `model.rs`, `terrain.rs`, `sky.rs`, `tree.rs`, `tree_texture.rs`, `effect.rs`, `effect_pipeline.rs`, `renderer.rs`, `camera.rs`, `ui.rs`, `shaders/model.wgsl`, `shaders/terrain.wgsl`, `shaders/effect.wgsl`, `shaders/ui.wgsl`), `crates/m2-cli/src/view.rs`, `mapview.rs` (with `map_folder.rs` for asset folders' maps), `trees.rs`, `effects.rs` and `window.rs` (`openm2 view model`, `view map`, `view tree`, `view effect`), `play.rs`, `actors.rs`, `fight.rs` and `world_effects.rs` (`openm2 play`), `floors.rs` (the buildings' floors, for both), `m2_assets::texture_paths`, `m2_assets::actor`, `m2_assets::effect`. Model and motion scripts: `m2_formats::script` (`formats/script.md`); maps: `m2_formats::map`, `m2_assets::map`; effects: `formats/effect.md`.

**Checked on:** a TMP4 40250 client, on an NVIDIA GPU (Vulkan) and on WARP (Windows' software adapter): characters put together from their model scripts (armours, skins, hair, cloaks) and monsters, in their wait, run, attack, damage and fall animations; `metin2_map_a1` from above matches the client's own minimaps (terrain, water, every building in place and turned the same way) and draws correctly up close with fog; effects (hits, fires, warp gates, smoke, waterfalls) play where the maps and actors place them; buildings' floors lie exactly on their models (`view map --floors`); in `play`, characters and monsters stand where the server puts them, fight, fall and get up again.

## World
- **Axes:** Z is up, right-handed, in centimetres (the files' `ArtToolInfo`: 100 units per metre, up `0 0 1`), as exported from 3D Studio MAX.
- **Facing:** animated characters and monsters face −Y.
- **Rest pose:** in the rest pose, the client's character skeletons turn the body to face +X. That is the Biped's bind pose; the animations turn it back.

## Posing
- **Local transform:** a bone's local transform is Granny's, applied in this order: scale/shear (3 × 3, stored by rows), then rotation (quaternion x y z w), then translation. As a matrix for column vectors: `T × R × S`.
- **World matrix:** the parent's world matrix times the local matrix. Parents come before children in the file.
- **Animation:**
  - A track moves the bone with the same name, ignoring ASCII case. Tracks in any track group count; a track group named like the skeleton is looked in first. Some animations hold the tracks of two objects with bones of the same names: the orc soldier's have a `Dummy01` group before the `Bip01` one its model and skeleton are named for, and its run drawn from the first lay on its back.
  - Animations come from the model layer (`m2_formats::model`), whatever file they were read from: each track's keys are sampled at the time, clamped to the animation's length. GR2's curves become keys when read (`formats/gr2.md`, "Curves" and "Into the model layer").
  - Keys hold their first value before the first key and their last after the last. Linear orientations turn along the shortest arc (slerp), as glTF asks; GR2's never are linear.
  - A track part without keys keeps the bone's rest value.
  - A sample that is not finite keeps the rest value too. Two animations in the client have NaN rotations.
  - Rotations are normalized after sampling.
- **Skinning matrix:** a bone's world matrix times its inverse rest world matrix (`InverseWorldTransform`, stored column-major).

**Curve evaluation is confirmed by eye.** The `run` cycle drawn as 8 poses alternates the legs smoothly, and the `wait` loop and the wolf's run look right. This answers the open question from the GR2 reader.

## Parts
A character is drawn from several models ("parts", GR2 or glTF): the body (armour), the hair, later weapons. Each is one model of its file, in the model layer (`Part::from_model`).
- **Joints:** vertex joints index the part's own skeleton, matched by name from the mesh's bone bindings. Joint `bones.len()` is an identity matrix, for meshes bound to a bone the skeleton lacks or to no bone.
- **Rigid meshes:** a mesh without bone weights follows its first bone binding with weight 1.
- **Weights:** they are normalized to add up to 1. Weights that add up to 0 make the vertex rigid.
- **Following the main skeleton:** the first part's skeleton is posed by the animation. Every other part takes, for each of its bones, the world matrix of the main skeleton's bone with the same name. Bones the main skeleton lacks keep their rest transform under their parent.
- **Palettes:** each part then uses its own inverse rest matrices, so parts exported with slightly different rest poses still fit.
- **Limits:** a part's skeleton has at most 255 bones (a palette of 256 matrices, 16 KiB, the downlevel uniform limit). The client's largest has 163 bones (`gr2 check`).

## Textures
- **Lookup:** a mesh's triangle groups name a material; its texture is the material's own or the first map's (diffuse first).
- **Paths:** GR2 files name textures by the artist's path. They are looked up in this order (`m2_assets::texture_paths`):
  1. the name as written, normalized (lowercase, `/`): `D:\ymir work\pc\warrior\warrior_4-1.dds` → `d:/ymir work/pc/warrior/warrior_4-1.dds`;
  2. its file name in the model's folder.
- **In the client:** `gr2 check` resolves 5,645 references the first way and 184 the second, and 62 (1%) are missing. The missing ones are mostly authoring leftovers: `.psd` and `.jpg` sources, lighting maps that were not shipped, and textures that model scripts replace.
- **Formats:** model textures are DDS (5,846 of 5,891 references); TGA, PSD and JPG are rare. DDS and TGA are read (`formats/tga.md`); TGA images get a mip chain of 2 × 2 averages. Baseline JPEG is read too (`formats/jpeg.md`): the import converts an effect's JPEG texture when no DDS or TGA of it is there.
- **From an asset folder** (`docs/specs/assets.md`): glTF files name their images by a path relative to the file, or hold them (`formats/gltf.md`). They are PNG, of any colour type and depth, made RGBA8 (`m2_render::png::decode_any`), with a mip chain of 2 × 2 averages, as TGA images. A DDS texture's own levels are not in its PNG, so minified textures look a little different from the client's (`assets.md`, "Pictures from the client and from the folder").
- **Skins:** a model script's shape or hair style replaces the model's texture whose file name matches `SourceSkin` with `TargetSkin` (`formats/script.md`, "Race data"); an actor's skins do the same (`source`, `target`). `--texture` comes first, then the skins, then the model's own texture. A skin that is not in the client falls back to the model's own texture, with a note.
- **Missing textures:** a texture that cannot be found draws white, and `view model` prints a note.
- **Decoding:** all levels are decoded on the CPU to RGBA8 and uploaded with their mipmaps, so no adapter needs block-compression support.
- **Alpha:**
  - Alpha counts as coverage only for DXT1 and 1-bit masks: texels under 0.5 are discarded (alpha test, no blending).
  - TGA textures with alpha (32-bit, or 16-bit with an alpha bit) use it as coverage too.
  - DXT3 and DXT5 textures draw opaque, because the client's DXT3 character textures keep a gloss mask in their alpha (`formats/dds.md`). Their alpha is uploaded as it is, with the texture's coverage beside it: the shader discards only what is coverage, and reads the rest as the gloss mask ("Shine").
  - These rules are the model layer's `Alpha::AsFile`, GR2 models' textures. A glTF model's material says instead (`Alpha::Mask` for `MASK` and `BLEND`, coverage; `Alpha::Opaque` otherwise), and a skin drawn in its place keeps that (`TextureData::with_alpha`). `openm2 assets import` writes each texture's from its format by the rules above.

## Shine
Upgraded items shine, as the original client draws it (the original client's source; in our own words): its own scripts turn armour and weapon specular on (`constinfo.py`, `prototype.py`), and its item table gives each item's `specular`, 0 to 100.
- **What shines:** a part made to shine (`Renderer::set_shine`, 0 to 1, with a shine map from `Renderer::upload_shine_map`) shines on its textures marked to (`TextureData::with_shine`): a body armour on the textures its shape's skins bring (never the face, the hair or a bare body), a weapon on all of its. The shine is the item's `specular` over 100.
- **How:** the shine map's colour times the texture's alpha (its gloss mask) times the shine, added to the lit colour and capped at white; fog then applies as to the rest. The map is looked up by where the camera's view reflects off the surface (the reflection of the direction to the eye about the normal, along the picture's right and up: the camera-space reflection vector the client gives its second texture stage), scrolled along both axes by `SHINE_SCROLL`, 0.42 a second (the client's 0.007 a frame at its 60 frames a second), the map repeating. Parts given none reflect black, which adds nothing; a part's shine and map are shared by its instances.
- **Two maps:** body armours reflect `special/spheremap.jpg` (bright streaks, a progressive JPEG), weapons `special/spheremap01.jpg` (a soft chrome streak), the asset folder's `[shine]` `armour` and `weapon` (`assets.md`).
- **In `play`:** a player's armour (as its own shape only) and the weapon in each hand, by their items; the maps read the first time a part shines. The test world's items: 0 up to +3, then 30, 40, 50, 65, 80 and 100 for +4 to +9.
- **Checked** by `tests/gpu.rs` (a shining texture where its mask says, half its strength, an instance the same, a texture not marked never) and in `play`'s window on the test world's armours and swords.

## Lighting and colour
- **Like the 2004 original:** textures and the target stay in gamma space, with no sRGB conversion. A window surface that wants sRGB is drawn through a non-sRGB view.
- **Light:** colour = texture × min(ambient + diffuse × max(n · l, 0), 1). The defaults are ambient 0.55 and diffuse 0.6.
- **Light direction:** l comes from the camera, a little above and to its left, so the visible side is lit.
- **Both sides:** models are drawn without culling. The normal is flipped to face the camera, so thin cloth lights the same from both sides.
- **MSAA:** 4× when the adapter supports it, otherwise none.

## Camera
- **Orbit:** an orbit camera around the target: yaw 0 looks from −Y (the front of an animated model). Positive yaw turns counter-clockwise seen from above, and positive pitch raises the camera.
- **Framing:** `Camera::framing` puts the camera just far enough away for every corner of a box to be in view (5% margin), with a 30° vertical field of view. `view model` frames the box around all poses it draws, so the camera stays still while the model moves.
- **Projection:** right-handed, depth 0 to 1 (wgpu's range).
- **Picking:** `Camera::ray` gives the ray from the eye through a spot of the picture (x and y from −1 to 1); points on it project back onto that spot.

## Terrain
A map is drawn from its areas (`formats/map.md`): one mesh per area, the terrain textures blended per tile, the baked shadows, water, the buildings and dungeon blocks its objects name, and its trees ([below](#maps-trees)). A map of an asset folder (`assets.md`, "Maps") is read into the same: its heights and tiles as the client's grids, its shadows as the picture they make, its texture repeats in metres as the scale that gives them, its environment as the client's, so the terrain code drew both while `view map` read a client too, and draws the import the same way (`mapview::load_map`).

- **Mesh:** 129 × 129 vertices per area at the height map's heights, two triangles per cell (split from the north-west to the south-east corner). Normals come from the neighbouring heights; the height map's border gives the edge vertices their neighbours, so areas join without seams.
- **Splatting:** each pixel blends the textures of the four tiles nearest to it, by distance to the tile centres (bilinear), so textures fade into each other over one tile (1 m). A tile whose number the set lacks counts for nothing; where no tile has a texture the ground is grey. The original builds per-texture alpha maps instead; how it smooths them is not known.
- **On the GPU:** the texture set is a texture array, every texture scaled to one side (the largest of the set, a power of two up to 512) with mipmaps made on the CPU. Each area's tile map is an integer texture read with `textureLoad`.
- **Texture repeat:** a texture with scale s repeats every 3,200 / s cm (`m2_render::terrain::TEXTURE_SPAN`), along map x and y. 3,200 cm (16 cells) was chosen by eye: paving stones about 1.5 m wide, grass and dirt without visible repetition. The original's rule is not known (open question).
- **Shadows:** the colour is multiplied by the area's baked shadow map, one texel per tile, filtered.
- **Light:** as for models, with the environment's light ([below](#maps-light-and-fog)); terrain is lit from one side only (its normals face up).
- **Water:** each water cell is a flat square at its layer's height, drawn after everything else in one see-through colour (RGB 0.18 0.32 0.45, alpha 0.6, lit by the ambient and diffuse colours), without writing depth. The original's water textures and waves are not drawn.
- **Objects:** a building or dungeon block draws every model of its GR2 file (every scene of a glTF file), rigid, at its object's placement: the position raised by its height offset, then turned about z, y and x, in that order. One upload per model; each object is an instance sharing its buffers with its own palette (every matrix the placement).
- **Effects:** a map object of type `Effect` plays its effect script at its placement, round and round ([below](#effects)); an asset folder's object its effect file. `--no-effects` leaves them out.
- **Trees:** a map object of type `Tree` gets a tree grown from openm2's own recipes ([below](#maps-trees)).
- **Not drawn:** a map's sound spots are heard, not drawn ([audio.md](audio.md)).

### Floors
Buildings and dungeon blocks have floors that are not the terrain: a pavilion's platform, a bridge's deck, the ramps over steps, a dungeon's storeys. Their models' attribute files hold them as height planes, triangles in the model's space ([`formats/mdatr.md`](formats/mdatr.md)), and a glTF model holds them as meshes marked `openm2_floor` ([`formats/gltf.md`](formats/gltf.md), "Floors"); `crates/m2-cli/src/floors.rs` places them.
- **Reading:** when a map's objects are read, each building's or dungeon block's model has its attributes read once; the triangles of its height planes are placed with the object's placement (as the model is) and filed by 8 m squares. `play` prints how many triangles of how many objects it read.
- **Left out:** triangles standing on edge (under 1 cm² seen from above), ones with a coordinate that is not a number, and ones wider or longer than 1,024 m (the client's widest is 272 m).
- **The ground** at a point (`LoadedMap::ground_at`) is the highest floor over it, or the terrain where that is higher. Actors, items on the ground, the camera's target and clicks (`pick`) all use it, so a character on a raised platform or a bridge stands on it instead of sinking to the terrain under it.
- **openm2's own rule:** how the original chooses among floors is not known. Taking the highest one puts a character under a bridge onto its deck; the maps' walls ([`formats/map.md`](formats/map.md), "Attributes") keep characters out of most such places. The server knows nothing of heights.
- **`view map --floors`** draws the floors in magenta, 3 cm over where they are, both sides: to check them against the models.

### Maps: light and fog
- **Light:** from the map's environment script (`formats/script.md`, "Environment"). The direction towards the light is the opposite of `DirectionalLight.Direction`. Diffuse is the background light's diffuse times the material's diffuse; ambient is the background light's ambient times the material's ambient, plus the material's emissive colour. That follows Direct3D's fixed-function lighting; whether the original sets more ambient light than the script says is not known.
- **Fog:** `view map --fog` fades colours to the fog colour from `NearDistance` to `FarDistance` (50 to 200 m in the first map). Without `--fog` the fog colour is only the background, so a whole map can be seen from above.
- **Far plane:** a map view sets the far plane past the whole map (`Scene::far`).

### Maps: the sky
The environment's `SkyBox` (`formats/script.md`, "Environment") is drawn before everything else by a pipeline of its own (`sky.rs`, `shaders/sky.wgsl`): one triangle over the whole picture, each pixel coloured by where its view ray points. The rays come from the camera's axes (as `Camera::ray`'s): from an inverted view matrix they were off by enough to make the clouds low in the sky tremble as the camera moved. Depth is neither tested nor written, so the terrain, models, water and effects cover it, and the fog does not reach it. A sky with neither bands nor pictures is not drawn; the fog's colour stays behind the world. How the client's numbers are laid out in the sky is openm2's reading; the original was not compared (`docs/roadmap.md`, "Sky in the original").

- **Colour bands:** `List Gradient` holds two colours per band, its top and its bottom: first the `GradientLevelUpper` bands above the horizon, then the `GradientLevelLower` bands below it. The bands lie on the sides of a box around the camera, which reach as high and as low as they are far: the upper bands share the sides' upper half evenly, from its top edge (45° up) down to the horizon, the lower ones the lower half; each fades from its top colour to its bottom one. Above the sides is the highest band's top colour, below them the lowest band's bottom colour. openm2 measures the height around the camera rather than on the flat sides, so no corners show. The colours' alpha (0 in every file) and `Scale` (the box goes with the camera) are not used; at most 16 bands are drawn. In the first map: deep blue overhead, paler down to an almost white horizon, grey-blue below it.
- **Pictures:** a sky with `bTextureRenderMode 1` shows the six `…FaceFileName` pictures instead of the bands, on a box around the camera: the front faces south, the back north, the left side east and the right side west, each seen from inside with its top up; the top's lower edge meets the front's upper edge, and its left edge the left side's.
  - That is how the client's pictures join: along the four sides and the top their edges differ by about 1 in 255 (by 25 the other way round).
  - Which way the front faces comes from the light: in all four skies the brightest side is the one facing where the environment's light comes from (from the south in three of them, the front; from the east in one, the left side).
  - A missing picture (none of the four has a bottom one) shows the bands.
  - The pictures are scaled to one side (a power of two up to 2,048) and drawn without mipmaps, so the seams take no smaller level.
- **Clouds:** `CloudTextureFileName` names a picture of clouds, white on black, added over the bands or pictures (its colour times its alpha). It lies on a flat layer `CloudHeight` over the camera (300 m in most maps, 40 to 45 m in three others), reaching `CloudScale` from the camera each way (2 km) and going with the camera. openm2 bends it down to meet the horizon at its reach, like a dome: a ray rising at angle a meets it height / (tan a + height / reach) away along the ground, overhead when it points straight up and at the reach when it is level. A flat layer seen that low would squeeze the clouds into thin stripes. The layer fades out over the outer two fifths of its reach: in the first map from about 6° over the horizon down to it, so `play`'s camera, which cannot look up, shows whole clouds at the top of the picture when it is lowered. Read as the layer's whole width, 2 km would have started the clouds of a flat layer at 17°, where that camera never looks. The cloud height is over the camera, not over the map's zero: the ground of those three lies above their 40 to 45 m (from 44, 53 and 70 m up). The picture repeats `CloudTextureScale` times over the reach and drifts `CloudSpeed` repeats a second (0.004 in the first map: about 2 m a second, 300 m up). `CloudColor` is read and not used: its colours are 0 in most files and reddish in the third maps.
- **Time:** in `play` the clouds drift on the session's clock; the `view map` window counts from its opening, and its pictures show them where they are after `--effect-time` seconds.
- **Looking up:** `view map --above M` raises the point looked at, so a negative `--pitch` looks up at the sky from above the ground.

### Maps: trees
Trees are grown from openm2's own recipes (`tree.rs`, `tree_texture.rs`), never from the client's SpeedTree files. A tree property's `treefile` is read only for its name.

- **Choosing a recipe:** the book (`crates/m2-cli/trees.ron`, RON) has recipes by name and two lists of words. In the tree file's own name, lowercased and without its folders, the first word of `names` found picks the recipe (`palmetto` comes before `palm`), else `default`; the first word of `seasons` found picks the season (`winter`, `fall`, `autumn`, `flower`), else summer. The client's 24 species names map to 17 recipes.
- **Placing:** each recipe and season is grown four times (seeds from the recipe's name), once per map. A spot takes one of the four, a turn about z and a size from 90 to 110 %, all from a hash of its position, so a tree stays the same each time the map is read. Then it is placed like a building (position, height offset, the object's rotation).
- **A recipe:**
  - `form`: `Broadleaf`, `Conifer`, `Palm` or `Rosette`.
  - `height`: the range a tree's height is picked from, in cm. Radii and leaf sizes are for the tallest and shrink with it.
  - `trunk`: radius at the foot, taper (top over foot), lean and bend in degrees, the bark's colour and how deep its furrows are (0 to 1). The foot flares out by up to 40 %.
  - `crown`: where the branches start (over the height), the widest radius (over the height), and the outline: `Round` (widest in the middle), `Flat` (widest at the top), `Cone` (at the bottom), `Column`.
  - `branches`: how many, their angle from the trunk, how far they droop along their length (negative: up), and how many twigs each carries.
  - `leaves`: the kind (`Broad`, `Needles`, `Tuft`, `Frond`, `Blade`), colour, size and count, and for fronds and long leaves their width over their length.
  - `fall`, `winter`, `flowers`: another leaf or bark colour, blossoms, bare branches, snow. A season without a look is drawn as summer.
  - **Caps:** at most 64 branches, 16 twigs a branch, 64 leaves a twig; branches × (twigs + 1) × leaves at most 2,048; heights up to 100 m. A book outside them is refused with the reason. A tree has at most 65,536 vertices.
- **Growth:** seeded (SplitMix64), so the same recipe, season and seed grow the same tree.
  - Broadleaf trees and conifers: the trunk is a tapered tube (10 sides, 8 segments) to 85 % of the height (a conifer's to the top). Branches leave it from the crown's start upwards, each turned by the golden angle (137.5°) from the one before. Each reaches as far as the crown's outline allows at its height and bends by its droop (tubes of 6 sides, 5 segments). Twigs leave the branches at 40° (4 sides). The upper branches of a round crown stand up to 35 % more upright.
  - Leaves of broadleaf trees: clusters of three crossed cards, turned at random, along each twig's outer half and around the top of the trunk; seen from any side, from above too.
  - Leaves of conifers: needle sprays along each branch, two cards from the branch outwards (one flat, one tilted across), shorter towards the tip.
  - Palms: a curved trunk (8 sides) with fronds at its top, each a strip from the trunk outwards, sagging by its droop and raised along its middle.
  - Rosettes: no trunk; leaves arch out of the ground, the inner ones upright, sagging no more than they rise.
- **Textures,** drawn by code per recipe and season:
  - Bark: 128 × 256, tiling both ways, with furrows along it; palms' rings around it. It repeats every 60 cm around a trunk and 120 cm along it.
  - Leaves: 256 × 256 clusters of pointed leaves on stems, needle sprays, or fans of blades; fronds 128 × 512, long leaves 64 × 256. All are cut out by alpha. Blossoms (40 discs) or snow patches are drawn over them.
  - Transparent texels take the leaves' mean colour, so smaller levels do not darken their edges. Each mip level's alpha is scaled so the same share of texels passes the alpha test as on the first level, so far crowns do not thin out.
- **Light:** the crown's leaves have normals pointing out from its middle (a conifer's from its trunk), tipped up, so a crown is lit as one volume, darker on its far side. Those normals are 8 long, and the model shader lights normals longer than 4 as they are, instead of turning them towards the camera. Fronds, long leaves and bark are lit like other models.
- **Checked:** on `metin2_map_a1`, every tree stands on its baked shadow in the terrain. The shapes and colours are openm2's own.
- **Not drawn yet:** wind, simpler trees far away, tree shadows. Trees do not stop walks.
- **`view tree NAME`** grows a recipe without a client: `--season`, `--seed`, `--variants N` (a grid of seeds), `--trees FILE`, `--yaw`, `--pitch`, `--distance`. NAME may be a tree file's name, to see which recipe and season it picks.

## Effects
Effect scripts and mesh effects (`formats/effect.md`) are followed through time on the CPU (`m2_render::effect`) and drawn as triangles in the world by their own pipeline (`effect_pipeline.rs`, `shaders/effect.wgsl`). What the scripts' numbers mean is openm2's reading (`formats/effect.md`, "Numbers that choose a behaviour"); none of it was compared with the original client.

- **Playing:** an effect is played from its start (`EffectInstance`), moved on by the seconds of each frame with a *place* (the matrix from the effect's space to the world: where its origin is and how it is turned). Randomness comes from a seed, so the same seed plays the same way.
  - **Elements:** each `Particle` and `Mesh` block starts `StartTime` seconds in and moves between its position keys.
  - **Emitters:** through each cycle an emitter owes `TimeEventEmissionCountPerSecond` particles a second, born within the step at random moments, as long as fewer than `MaxEmissionCount` are alive (at most 2,000 per emitter); a full emitter does not save particles up. It runs its cycles (once, `LoopCount` times, or for ever) and stops.
  - **Birth:** at the element's position plus a point of its shape (on the edge with the flag; a circle's and a sphere's distance from the middle even up to the radius, the growing added), as the original client places them (its source); flying along the direction curves, plus away from (towards) the shape's centre at 100 for advanced type 1 (2), plus a share of `EmittingDirection`'s spread at random, all × the velocity. Its life, size, starting angle and spin are drawn then. A particle not attached is put into the world at birth and stays there; an attached one keeps the effect's space and goes where the effect goes.
  - **Moving:** each step its age grows, gravity pulls it down (cm/s²), air resistance takes away that part of its speed at each of the original's steps, 60 a second (its clock advances 16 or 17 ms at a time), it moves, and the emitter's angular velocity turns it round the point on the element's path it was born at, about the effect's up axis, by its degrees at each of those steps; it dies at the end of its life.
  - **Meshes:** a mesh element shows frame `time / MeshAnimationFrameDelay` of its file, round its loops, and is gone after them (for ever with loop count 0).
  - **Finished:** when every emitter has run its cycles and its particles died, and every mesh element played out. Effects that loop for ever never finish.
- **Drawing** (`EffectInstance::draw`, every frame): each particle is a quad, twice `size × TimeEventScale` across (the size is half of it, as the original draws it; facing the camera, `TimeEventSizeX` stands up the screen and `TimeEventSizeY` lies across it, its picture upright), coloured and faded by its curves over its life, turned by its spin or `TimeEventRotation`: facing the camera (billboard types 0, 1, 4, 5), upright turned towards the camera (2), or flat on the ground turned with the effect (3); a stretched particle lies along its last step instead, its Y size along it times one and the logarithm of one and the step's length in cm, its X size across as seen from the camera, straight up when it didn't move (the original's: a warrior's sword aura is a few such particles shot up the blade, a long flame). It shows the texture its animation type says. A mesh element draws each mesh of its frame at the element's position, turned towards the camera for billboard types 1 and 2, its alpha the frame's visibility (cut to 0–1) × `TimeEventAlpha` × the colour's alpha. Particles and meshes whose texture is not in the client are not drawn.
- **Pipeline:** world-space triangles (position, texture coordinate, colour, the texture operation) in runs by texture and blending, uploaded each frame. The fragment combines texel and colour by the Direct3D texture operation (texture times colour for 4, twice for 5, …). Each blend factor pair gets a pipeline the first time it is drawn, by Direct3D's factor numbers (one, source alpha, …); the target's alpha is kept. Depth is tested against the world but not written, so effects hide behind buildings and characters and do not hide each other; elements draw in their order, effects in the order they were added. Effects are drawn after the water, from both sides. In fog they fade out with distance (their colour and alpha go to 0) instead of taking the fog's colour, so glows do not light the fog up. Textures keep their alpha whatever it is (DDS or TGA, with mipmaps), and sample repeating (mesh effects' texture coordinates go past 1).
- **Map effects:** `view map` and `play` play each placed effect at its object's placement, round and round (a new seed each time it has played out), each a different way into its playing so they do not all start together (`view map --effect-time`, default 3 s, before its picture; 1 s in `play` and the map window). The map window and `play` move and draw only those within 100 m (`play`: 80 m) of the point looked at.
- **In `play`** (`world_effects.rs`; openm2's rules):
  - **States:** an entity whose `affects` shows poisoned, burning, slowed or stunned (`protocol/openm2/entities.md`) plays the loop the client's scripts register for it (`playersettingmodule.py`), round and round, while the state lasts, on the bone they name, turning with it: `effect/hit/blow_poison/poison_loop.mse` and `effect/hit/blow_flame/flame_loop.mse` on `Bip01`, `effect/affect/slow.mse` from the feet, `effect/etc/stun/stun_loop.mse` on `Bip01 Head`; an asset folder's `[effects]` `poisoned`, `burning`, `slowed` and `stunned`.
  - **Skills on players**, as the client's scripts register them and its source attaches them (the original client's source): each skill an EntitySkills names on a player (`protocol/openm2/entities.md`, "Skills on players") plays its look from the asset folder's skills file (`[skill.look]`) round and round while it is on, on its bone turning with it (from the feet without one, or on a model without that bone): a warrior's sword aura `pc/warrior/effect/geom_sword_loop.mse` on `equip_right_hand` (`geom_spear_loop.mse` while it holds a two-handed weapon); a strong body's `gyeokgongjang_loop.mse`; a ninja's light step and a shaman's swiftness (`gyeonggong_loop.mse`, `10kwaesok_loop.mse`) only while it moves; a sura's enchanted blade `gwigeom_loop.mse` on `Bip01 R Finger2`, its fear, enchanted armour, dark protection and flame spirit (`fear_loop.mse`, `jumagap_loop.mse`, `heuksin_loop.mse`, `muyeong_loop.mse`); a shaman's blessing and reflection (`3hosin_loop.mse`, `boho_loop.mse`), dragon's strength on `Bip01 R Hand` and attack up on `Bip01 L Hand`. Not on a player hidden from us, as its other effects. Not yet: the aura's longer reach and trail in the original's client. A hidden player (`affects` bit 4) is not drawn for others, nor its effects, its name, its minimap mark or its body for picking; our own character stays drawn.
  - **Glows of upgraded items**, as the client's own scripts register them (`playersettingmodule.py`) and its source picks them (the original client's source): `sword_7.mse` to `sword_9.mse` on swords and two-handed weapons, `_s` on daggers (in each hand) and bells, `_f` on fans, `_b` on bows (in the left hand), and `armor_7.mse` to `armor_9.mse` on body armours, at `Bip01`. A player wearing an item whose glow level (the item table's `glow`) the asset folder's `[[effects.upgraded]]` has a glow for plays it round and round: the highest such level's, on its bone (the armour's on `Bip01`, a weapon's on each hand bone holding it), **turned as the bone is**, as the client attaches them. The client's level is the last digit of the item's number plus its table's socket value less 3 (`m2_assets::items::client_glow`): a basic Sword+9 (1 socket) shows level 7, Sword+7 and +8 none, a Bloody Sword+9 (3) level 9. The glow changes as soon as the item does, and goes with it.
  - **Hits:** every Damage but a miss, a heal and what poison and fire take plays the client's general hit effect (`effect/hit/blow_1/blow_1.mse`; a critical hit `effect/hit/blow_3/15.mse`) with its own centre (`BoundingSpherePosition`: the hit effect's sparks are made 1.2 m above its origin) at the surface of the body of the entity hit (its cylinder) on the side of the one who hit (our character, or for hits on us the camera), 60 % of its height up, turned so what flies off flies away from them. The weapons' item scripts name hit effects this client does not have, and the monsters' lists are empty (`formats/effect.md`), so which effect plays is openm2's choice.
  - **Attached effects:** an actor's model script's effects (`AttachingData` type 2) play for as long as it shows, at its bone's position (or its feet) plus their offset turned by their rotation, in the actor's axes: the bone's own turn is not used, as for collision spheres (openm2's reading).
  - **Smoke:** a metin stone plays the stage of its smoke for the quarters of its health it lost (the server tells its health while it is our target; stage 0 before), at its smoke bone.
  - **Motion events:** when an actor's motion (standing, running, or one played once) passes an effect event's time, its effect starts (an event timed after the motion's end never comes, as for sounds: `audio.md`) at the event's bone (or its feet) plus its position, **turned as the bone is** (its axes, not its scale); with `FollowingEnable` it stays on the bone, otherwise where it started. In the client these are skills, fishing and bosses' attacks; no warrior's combo step and no common monster's motion plays one. **A fishing event** (`FishingEffectFlag`) plays instead at the fisher's float, unturned, on the water's surface there (the terrain where there is none), in place of the last one there: the float the server last sent ([`ui.md`](ui.md), "Fishing"), as the original client places it. Turning with the bone is openm2's reading of how the client's sword glows are made: Sword Spin's and Aura of the Sword's (on the warrior's `equip_right_hand`) and the sura's Enchanted Blade (on `Bip01 R Hand`) move their emitter about a blade's length (110 to 140 cm) along the effect's z, and a weapon hangs on its bone unturned, its blade along the bone's z; drawn in the actor's axes, the glow would rise straight up from the hand. The body bones' effects are particles at the bone, most of them not attached to it, which look alike either way; `Bip01 Footsteps` keeps the actor's axes.
  - **Items on the ground:** each plays the twinkle the client's program names (`effect/etc/dropitem/dropitem.mse`) over it for as long as it lies there.
  - **Rings**, as the client's scripts register them (`playersettingmodule.py`) and its source attaches them (the original client's source): the living entity under the pointer (over the world, not the windows; never our own character, as picking) plays `effect/etc/click/click_select.mse` round and round from its feet, and our living target `click_glow_select.mse`; the dead show neither. An asset folder's `[effects]` `hovered` and `targeted`. Each ends as soon as the pointer leaves it, or the target changes or dies.
  - **Potions and levels**, as the client's scripts register them (`playersettingmodule.py`) and its source attaches them (the original client's source): an EntityEffect (`protocol/openm2/entities.md`, "Effects") plays its effect once on the entity, from its feet without a bone, moving and turning with it until it is played out (`WorldEffects::once`), its sounds with it: `effect/etc/recuperation/drugup_red.mse` for the health a potion gave back, `drugup_blue.mse` for its mana (a short flash rising from the feet, with small rings floating up), `effect/etc/levelup_1/level_up.mse` for a level gained (beams of light over a glow at the feet, about 1.8 s). An asset folder's `[effects]` `health_potion`, `mana_potion` and `level_up`. It starts in the frame after the message, where the entity is drawn then; not on a player hidden from us, as its other effects. The original shows none on an invisible character, its own too; openm2 draws its own hidden character with its effects.
  - **Flying effects:** see "Flying effects" below.
  - Effects of an actor that leaves go with it; a game's effects go at the logout.
- **From an asset folder:** an effect file ([`assets.md`](assets.md), "Effects") converts to the same script the renderer plays (`EffectFile::to_effect`), its mesh effects read from glTF into the same mesh files (`Gltf::mesh_effect`), its textures from PNG (with mip levels the renderer makes, as for models); an empty texture name draws white, as a texture the client lacks. A hit's effect and the twinkle over items are the ones `assets.toml`'s `[effects]` names.
- **`openm2 view effect <file.effect.toml> --assets <dir> --screenshot out.png`** plays an asset folder's effect at the origin in steps of 1/60 s and draws it at `--time` seconds (default 0.3), or `--frames N` moments spread over `--duration` seconds in a grid, on a dark background; the camera frames its bounding sphere (`--distance` in metres, `--yaw`, `--pitch`), `--seed` changes the randomness. It prints how many particles are alive and whether the effect finished.

## Flying effects
What monsters shoot, and the experience a kill gave (`world_effects.rs`; the client's flying effects, `formats/effect.md`, "Flying effects", moved as the original client's source moves them; the protocol's Projectile and Flight, `protocol/openm2/combat.md`, "Monsters that shoot" and "Flights"):
- **When:** a Projectile tells `play` what a monster shot at. When the motion the monster then plays (its attack) passes a fly event's time ([`formats/script.md`](formats/script.md), "Motion events"), the event's flying effect leaves from its bone (or the feet) plus its position, at that target. A monster that shot at nothing flies nothing; the last target counts until it shoots again or leaves.
- **The experience**: a Flight of kind 0 flies the asset folder's `[flies]` `experience` from the feet of the monster killed (its body, which lies there) at the one who gained it: the client's golden pieces (`effect/etc/gathering/ga_piece_yellow_small2.msf`, as its scripts register them). From a monster we no longer have a body for, nothing flies.
- **Its way:** its point is where its target is to be hit: the same point as hits show, 60% of the target's height up, on the target's axis (`show_hits`); it is followed while we see the target, after that the last one seen. It has a velocity and an acceleration (cm/s and cm/s², the scene's axes):
  - **Leaving:** its velocity is `InitialVelocity` along its forward −Y, and its acceleration the file's, both in its own axes, turned first by its launch turn (`launch_turn`) and then by the turn from −Y to its point. The launch turn is the original's: about its up axis (+Z) by `ConeAngle`, then about its own Y by `RollAngle` less 90°; one that spreads (`SpreadingFlag`) first turns about its up axis by the mean of two even draws from −60° to 60°, then about its forward axis by any angle (drawn from a seed of its own). So the client's arrows (cone 0) leave straight at it, and the golden pieces (cone 90°, spreading, leaving back at 1 m/s and sped up 5 m/s² the other way) leave down out of the body, turn round and rise from it, apart.
  - **Each frame:** a homing one, from `HomingStartTime`, turns its velocity and acceleration towards the point by at most twice `HomingMaxAngle` for each of the client's steps (60 a second; from 90° at once); then the acceleration, and `Gravity` upwards, change the velocity, and it moves by it.
- **Its axes:** its launch turn, then turned towards its point on its first step, and turned as its velocity is when it homes in (`Flight::turn`). Its attached effects also spin about them by `AngularVelocity` (degrees a second about its y, then x, then z, `spin_step`): the golden pieces' 450° a second about y, its forward axis.
- **Its end:** when the step it made passes within its bomb range of the point (at least 30 cm), it ends and its bomb effect plays with its origin there, unturned, as the original's does; past its range it ends with nothing. Its attached effects and tails end with it.
- **Its looks** (as the original places them, `attached_place`): each attached effect plays round and round. Fly type 1 is on the flying point, in its axes; the others are in its spun axes, away from it along (−sin `Roll`, 0, −cos `Roll`): type 2 by `Distance` (the golden pieces': 20 cm, so with the spin they go round their way), type 3 by `Amplitude` × sin(2π t / `Period`), type 4 by `Amplitude` × u e^−u (u = t / `Period`), t the seconds since it left.
- **Tails** (`TailFlag`, as the original draws them): where the attached effect was in the last `TailLength` seconds, a ribbon through those points facing the camera, `TailSize` cm to each side, narrowing to nothing at its oldest (with `TailShapeRect` it keeps its size), in `TailColor` (its alpha) added to what is behind it, without a texture, depth tested; each piece between two points has a tip ahead of the newer point and one behind the older (`Trail`).
- **Not yet:** hitting anything on the way: the server decides the hit.
- **`openm2 view effect <file.fly.toml> --assets <dir> --screenshot out.png`** flies it from 1 m up at a target `--target` metres ahead (default 10), seen from its side, and draws `--time` or `--frames`/`--duration` moments of the flight, then says when it reached its target.

## Playing
`openm2 play` (`crates/m2-cli/src/play.rs`) is the game in a window: the session runs on its own thread (`m2_net::Net`, commands through a `Remote`), and every frame the window draws a copy of the session's world at the session's clock (`docs/specs/protocol/openm2/movement.md`).

What it reads comes from an asset folder (`--assets`; `crates/m2-cli/src/source.rs`): its maps, actors, motions, effects, interface, items and skills ([`assets.md`](assets.md), "`play --assets`"). A player's client gets there through `openm2 assets import`, which converts the client's files named below into them.

- **The map:** `assets.toml`'s maps with a place give the map that holds our character's world position (the import places them as the client's atlas does); the areas within `--radius` metres (default 400) of the character are read, with buildings. When the character goes more than half that radius from where they were read, or onto another map, they are read again. A world position becomes a scene position by subtracting the map's corner and turning y round (scene y points north).
- **Actors:** every entity is drawn with a model from its actor ([`assets.md`](assets.md), "Actors"), which says what the client's model script and motion list say (`formats/script.md`):
  - a player by its class and gender's model script: the shape its body armour gives (`script.md`, "Worn items"; else the base outfit, `BaseModelFileName`), the hair style whose index is the entity's hair value, else the first, and its weapon's model held at the hand bone: the model's own skeleton in its rest pose, carried by that bone of the posed body (`Pose::held`). A change of clothes (an EntityUpdate with other parts) draws the entity with its new model; the motion goes on;
  - anything else by its race through `assets.toml`'s monsters (the import's, from the monster list); an entity without a model is not drawn (noted once).
  - Each model is read and uploaded once; each entity is an instance with its own palette.
- **Placement:** on the ground under its position ([floors](#floors): the terrain's height there, or a building's floor over it), turned about z by its direction: 0° faces south, the way the models face in their files (−Y), and the angle turns counter-clockwise seen from above, like the server's.
- **Moves without a time:** a move the server gives no duration (openm2's server times every walk, `protocol/openm2/movement.md`) would put the actor at its new point at once. `play` runs it there instead, from where it was drawn, at its run motion's speed (450 cm/s without one) times its movement speed over 100, playing its run motion at that pace (`chase` in `play.rs`). Timed walks, our own character and the dead are drawn where the session puts them; a jump of more than 30 m is taken at once. Picking and hits use the drawn place.
- **Motions:** standing, an actor plays its wait motion (players: `general/wait.msa` beside the base model, or the held weapon's folder's, `onehand_sword/wait.msa`; monsters: `WAIT` from their motion list); walking, its run motion (`run.msa` in the same folder; `RUN`, else `WALK`). The run motion plays as fast as the actor moves, so the feet keep up: the walk's speed over the motion's own (its `Accumulation` over its duration), between a quarter and four times.
- **Fighting:** over standing and running, an actor plays a motion once when the server says so (`formats/script.md`, "Attack motions"; `crates/m2-cli/src/play.rs`, `next_once`):
  - an attack move (EntityMove `kind` 2 or 3, `protocol/openm2/combat.md`): a combo move's step (its `argument`, from 1) from the weapon's combo, else a single attack (monsters: `NORMAL_ATTACK` or a numbered variant). It starts as far into the motion as the move is old, so a move older than its motion plays nothing;
  - Damage to the actor (we see only the hits we deal and take): a flinch, unless it is attacking;
  - its death (EntityDied, or met dead): the fall, which stays in its last pose until the actor is alive again (our restart).
  - a skill move (EntityMove `kind` with bit 7, `protocol/openm2/skills.md`): the skill of the entity's job with that motion number (below, "Our fighting", "Skills"), like an attack (it starts as far in as the move is old; damage does not stop it);
  - Walking ends any of them but the fall; a motion played out goes back to standing. Variants are picked by their motion list weight (players' alike), from the entity's id and the time.
  - The motions play in place: the client's attack motions do not move the root (`formats/script.md`, "Motion data").
- **Picking:** each entity is an upright cylinder around where it stands: its radius a quarter of its model's width plus depth, its height the model's top, both from the box around its wait motion's poses (8 samples; 40 by 150 cm without a model). A left press casts `Camera::ray` through the cursor and takes the nearest entity whose cylinder's box it meets, never our own character, unless an item on the ground is under it (below).
- **Collision spheres:** an entity also has the collision spheres of its model script (`formats/script.md`, "Race data"), all of them, whatever their `CollisionType`: placed where they are while it stands (from its feet, or from their bone in the first pose of its wait motion, the offset in the model's axes), turned as it faces. They do not move with its motions. Our swings hit them (below).
- **Our fighting** (`crates/m2-cli/src/fight.rs`; openm2's own rules, as the original's are not known):
  - **Going for a foe:** a left press on a monster or metin stone makes it the foe: it is chosen (`Command::Target`, the target window shows it, `ui.md`), and we swing at it while the button is held, once for a click. On anyone else a press only chooses them. Space swings while held: at the foe, else at the target if it can be fought, else where we face.
  - **Targets by hits**, as the original client's source does it: every monster or metin stone our swing or skill hits is chosen (`Command::Target`, before its Hit), so the target window and its ring show what we fight, also when Space swung where we face. The target is held: for 2 s after a press chose it, for 1 s after a hit chose it, and each hit on the target holds it 1 s more; a hit on another while it is held leaves it as it is (`fight::TARGET_HELD`, `TARGET_HELD_BY_HAND`). A click on the ground walks there and leaves the fight; a foe that dies or leaves ends it (a step asked for in the last swing's window is not swung), and so does our death.
  - **Swinging:** with a weapon that has combo motions, the chain runs `combo_01` to `combo_04` (openm2's chain, `docs/roadmap.md`, "Combo steps"); bare-handed (no combo motions), the single attacks. A swing is `Command::Attack` facing the foe (the combo step, or 0), so the world shows our own attack move and its motion plays as anyone's (above, "Fighting"; a single attack's variant is picked from our id and the move's start, the same for the picture and the hits). While a step plays, the button held when its `ComboInputData` window is open (`PreInputTime` to `InputLimitTime`) asks for the next step, which starts at `DirectInputTime`; a step asked for in its window comes even if the button was let go after. A step that played out without that (and `combo_04`, whose window is empty) starts the chain again from step 1 while the button is held.
  - **Walking up:** a chain is swung from where all its steps reach. Its reach is the shortest of its steps' (`MotionData::reach`; 150 cm without hit data; a single attack's own without a combo). Before a chain's first swing, a foe whose edge (its cylinder) is farther than 70 % of the chain's reach is walked up to around walls, to half the chain's reach from its edge along the path's last leg (a last leg shorter than that is left out); the walk is aimed again when the foe moved 1 m from where it was aimed at. A foe the walls keep us from is walked to as near as they let us, and not again until it moves 1 m. The later steps swing while the foe's edge is in their own reach; a foe that got farther is walked up to, and the chain starts again.
  - **Hits:** while a swing plays, every 1/60 s of it since the last frame, its hits are placed where our model is drawn: a hit's line from the striking bone to the weapon's tip (`Hit::segment_at`) touches an entity when it passes through one of its collision spheres; an attack event's spheres (`EnableHitProcess` not 0) touch those whose spheres they reach. An entity without spheres is touched in its cylinder instead (a line seen from above, at a height inside it). Each monster or metin stone touched is told with `Command::Hit` once a swing; our own character and the dead are never hit. Nothing else (the distance, the hit limit) is checked here: the server checks them (`protocol/openm2/combat.md`).
  - **Skills:** a skill used (`ui.md`, "Skills") is `Command::UseSkill`, so the world shows our own skill move and its motion plays as anyone's. Its motion is the skill's at our grade (`formats/script.md`, "Skill descriptions": the grade's own motion script, else another grade's or `_me`); other players' at normal grade, which the move does not say. Its attack spheres and hit lines hit as a swing's do, but each monster once a strike, not once a motion (Three-Way Cut's three cuts each hit), told with `Command::Hit` and the skill's number. Its effects and sounds are its motion script's ([above](#effects)). A skill motion's `LoopData` is not read: it plays once through.
  - **Damage numbers**, as the original client draws them (its scripts and its source): a hit on our character or on our target shows its amount over the entity's head (its cylinder's top), an effect a digit (at most 8) played once with that digit's picture in place of its own (`WorldEffects::play_retextured`), the digits 30 cm apart along the screen, the last over the head, the effect's x along the screen to the right: the asset folder's `[numbers]`, the client's `effect/affect/damagevalue/`: `damage.mse` and `damage_1.mse` in turn with the red `damage_0.dds` to `_9` on us, `target.mse` with the yellow `target_0.dds` to `_9` on our target. Each pops up from the head, grows and fades in half a second. A dodge or a block shows `miss.mse` (a red `Miss`) on us and `target_miss.mse` (a yellow one) on whoever else it names. A hit on another, a heal and nothing taken show nothing; critical, piercing, poison and fire show as any hit. A folder without `[numbers]` shows none. A miss, a heal and what poison and fire take don't make the entity flinch.
- **Names over characters** (`crates/m2-cli/src/nametags.rs`): as in the original, a character's name shows over its head, with a player's level before it; the colours, the reach and the words are openm2's.
  - **Who:** each living player (ours too), NPC, monster and metin stone within 50 m of our character. Nothing over the dead, over what has no name yet (a player before its EntityInfo, a monster the asset folder doesn't name), or over portals, doors, buildings and horses.
  - **What:** players' names and levels are the server's EntityInfo's ([`protocol/openm2/entities.md`](protocol/openm2/entities.md)), NPCs' names too (else the asset folder's monster names); monsters' and metin stones' names and levels are `assets.toml`'s `[[monster]]` ones, as in the target window (the server sends none). `Lv 12` comes before the name in gold, left out at level 0 (NPCs); the name is light for players, light blue for NPCs, light red for monsters and stones.
  - **A game master's mark**: over the name of a player whose `state` has flag 2 ([`protocol/openm2/entities.md`](protocol/openm2/entities.md)), ours too, a line (15 pixels) higher: the client's own picture, the skin's look `game_master` (`locale/en/effect/ymirred.tga`), 64 by 33 pixels (times the interface's scale), standing on that line (a label's picture, `ui.md`). It fades in and out as the client's effect script for it (`gm.mse`) has its one particle do, openm2's reading: over 2.216 s, opaque 0, 0.77, 1, 0.79 and 0 at 0, 10.8, 20.8, 90.3 and 99.7 % of it, straight between, then again; every mark together. An asset folder without the picture (imported before import 6) shows `GM` in amber (255, 185, 40) instead, steady.
  - **Where:** 20 cm over the entity's cylinder (above, "Picking"), centred, as a label (`ui.md`: the level its lead); the farthest are drawn first, so nearer names cover them.
  - **Turned off** with the Settings window's Show names (`ui.md`, "The game menu"), kept in the player's settings ([`client.md`](client.md), "The player's settings").
- **Items on the ground** (`crates/m2-cli/src/ground.rs`; how the original shows them is not known, so these are openm2's rules):
  - **Model:** the one the item table names for the item (the import's, from the item list: `formats/script.md`, "Item list"), else the bag it names (the import names the one the client's program does, `d:/ymir work/item/etc/item_bag.gr2`); each read once, drawn in its rest pose, one instance per item. Without an item table, items have no model and are picked by their cylinder alone.
  - **How it lies:** a weapon (item type 1), made standing on its grip with the blade up, is laid flat: turned 90° about its x axis, the middle of its box on the item's spot and its underside on the ground (the client's sword lies 1.36 m long and 11 cm high). Anything else stands as made: the client's bag, gold and potions are made standing on their origin. It lies on the ground under the item's position (the server sends no height), turned about z by an angle from its id, so items side by side lie differently.
  - **Names:** over each item, 15 cm over its box, the item table's name as a label (`ui.md`): light grey, darker grey when someone else owns it. Items within 1 m of the first of a group (by id) lie together: their names go over the tallest of them, stacked 15 pixels apart (times the interface's scale), so a monster's drops on one spot stay readable.
  - **Picking:** each item is an upright cylinder around it as it lies (as wide as half its longer side, at least 25 cm; at least 30 cm high). A left press takes, as the original client does (its source; facts only): an item the ray meets, else the item whose name is under the pointer (`Ui::label_at`, the name's line of text), else the entity. So an item is picked up even where it lies in a body: a monster's drops land within 50 cm of where it died, in its body's cylinder, which stays the 5 s its corpse does.
  - **Picking up:** a left press on an item leaves any fight and walks to 50 cm short of it along the path's last leg (`Command::MoveTo`, around walls, aimed again only when not walking there); once our character is within 1.5 m it stops and asks for it (`Command::PickUp`). An item the walls keep us from is asked for once our character is as near as they let it if that is within 4.5 m (the server's 5 m, less a margin for where it has us: `ground::ASK_REACH`), else given up. The item leaving, our death, a click on the ground or an entity, or Space end it. The server takes an item within 5 m (`protocol/openm2/ground-items.md`).
  - **Dropping:** an item of the bag carried by the pointer and let go of over the world is asked about first (`ui.md`, "The inventory").
- **Walking:** a left click (the mouse moving under 5 pixels between press and release, not on an entity or an item) casts `Camera::ray` through the cursor and walks to where it first meets the ground (`LoadedMap::pick`: steps of 1 m, then halving to under 1 cm; floors count, [below](#floors)), around walls (below). Walks are timed with the run speed the server gave ([`protocol/openm2/movement.md`](protocol/openm2/movement.md), "Speed"); the run motion plays at its own pace.
- **Walking around walls** (`crates/m2-cli/src/walls.rs`): when the map is first read, its areas' attribute files become a walk mask of the whole map, a square a metre, blocked where flag `0x01` is set or no attribute file covers it (the import's rule, [`server/content.md`](server/content.md), "Walk masks"; the number of blocked squares is printed). Every walk of ours (a click, `--walk`, walking up to a foe, an item or an NPC) is a path from `m2_content::WalkMap::find_path`, walked a leg at a time by the session ([`protocol/openm2/movement.md`](protocol/openm2/movement.md), "Paths"):
  - a point in a blocked square gives way to the first open spot on the line back from it to us, every 25 cm;
  - where no path is found (none in the search box, which is at most 512 m on a side), the walk goes as far along the straight line as it is clear, and nowhere when that is under 10 cm;
  - a map without attribute files is open everywhere: walks go straight (noted once).
  - The server checks every move against the same walls (its content's masks, imported from the same flag). A refused move puts our character where the server has it (`Event::MoveRefused`, [`protocol/openm2/movement.md`](protocol/openm2/movement.md), "Refused moves"), and what the walk went for is not walked to again from there: an item or an NPC is given up, a foe is waited for until it moves; a scripted `--walk` goes on to the next.
- **Walking with the keys** (`crates/m2-cli/src/steer.rs`, openm2's rules): W, A, S and D, or the arrow keys, run away from the camera, towards it and to its sides, and between two of them held together (keys that undo each other count for nothing). They walk straight lines, not paths:
  - each walk ends 5 m ahead, or at the last spot before the first wall on the line (`Walls::reach`: every 25 cm, as long as the straight walk there is clear). The next is asked for when less than 2 m of it is left, or when the keys and the camera point 8° away from where they did, but never within 0.1 s of the last;
  - a way walled within 1 m gives way to one turned 25°, else 50°, to either side, the farther of the two at the nearest turn that goes 1 m: a wall met at a slant is slid along, one met head on stops the character;
  - letting go of every key stops the walk where the character is (`Command::Stop`);
  - a new press leaves any fight, pick-up or talk. While a foe is gone for, the attack is held or a swing plays, an item or an NPC is walked up to, a skill's motion plays or the scripted walks are under way, the keys wait, and walk again once it is over. They do nothing while the character is dead or a field has the keyboard;
  - a refused walk ends where the server put the character, and the next starts there.
- **Camera:** it orbits a point 1.2 m above our character's feet: right drag turns it, the wheel zooms (3 to 100 m), F switches the fog, Escape logs out. A window that loses the focus lets go of the attack and the movement keys. `--distance` (15 m), `--yaw` (0: looking north) and `--pitch` (35°) set where it starts.
- **Without a window:** `--screenshot out.png` plays headless and draws one picture `--stay` seconds (default 2) after our character stands in the world and any `--walk DX,DY` walks (metres east and south) are done, then logs out; `--inventory` opens the inventory first. `--say TEXT` (with or without a window) says a chat line once in the world, before the walks, such as `/skillgroup 1` (openm2's server has no GM commands yet). `--fight`, once the walks are done, makes the nearest living monster or metin stone the foe as if the button were held on it, then the next when it dies; `--pick-up`, once the walks and any fight are done, goes for the nearest item on the ground that is not someone else's as if pressed on, then the next (each once); `--skill SKILL`, once those and any talk are done and the server told our skills (or 2 s later), chooses the nearest monster and uses the skill on it from where it stands, once. The picture is still taken `--stay` seconds after the walks. The window title and the line printed with the picture say where the character is.
- **Light:** the map's environment, as in `view map`; characters are lit like the terrain for now (the environment's separate character light is not used yet).
- **Effects:** the map's, hits, actors' attached effects and smoke, their motions' effects, the states' loops and the items' twinkle ([above](#effects)). `--after-hit S` (hidden) takes the picture S seconds after the first hit effect instead of `--stay` after the walks.
- **Before the game:** the login, character and loading screens (`ui.md`, "Play's screens") are the interface over a plain clear. On the select and create screens the character is drawn into its own target (an `Offscreen` of the window's format, the size of the layout's `@character` node) that the interface shows: the armour (the character list's body part) and hair as in the world, without a weapon (the list does not say), playing its wait motion, cleared see-through; the camera looks at its front (yaw 0, 8° up), framing the box around the motion's poses (8 samples) with 10% room. The map loads once the loading screen was drawn.
- **`--screenshot --screen login|select|game`** draws that screen: the login screen needs no server; `select` logs in, waits `--stay` seconds on the select (or create) screen once the character is drawn, and logs out without picking one.

## Riding
openm2's own. A player whose EntityMount names a look rides: its actor key asks for the riding motions (`load_player` with `Look::riding`: the weapon's riding folder, `horse_<weapon>` for its attacks, then `horse` for standing, running, flinching and falling; no job skills), and its mount's model (the look's monster actor) is drawn under it at its place and turn, standing or running as the rider moves, at the rate its feet keep up. The rider sits at the mount's `saddle` bone (`m2_assets::actor::SEAT_BONE`): where the bone is in the mount's pose, not how it turns, so it rises and falls with the gallop; a mount without the bone has its rider at 70 % of its standing height. The rider's standing and running are in step with the mount's: the same part of their motions. Our camera rises by the seat's height. Found by experiment on an import (`view model ACTOR --rider PLAYER --rider-motion horse/run --seat saddle`): the riding motions are made to sit on the origin, the three horse families and the four classes sit right at the bone. Name tags and picking keep the rider's standing box (not raised yet).

## Interface
The interface (`ui.md`) is drawn after the world, over the resolved picture, by its own pipeline (`m2_render::UiRenderer`):

- **Input:** an `m2_ui::DrawList` (triangles with positions in pixels, texture coordinates, an RGBA8 colour and their quad's texture rectangle, in batches by texture and clip rectangle) and the glyph atlas pages.
- **Pipeline:** no depth and no multisampling; the pixel position becomes clip space from the target's size; colour = texel × vertex colour, blended over what is there (source alpha, one minus source alpha), in gamma space like the rest.
- **Textures:** the white texel for flat colour; images the application uploaded under its own numbers (RGBA8, one level; uploaded again into the same texture when they change at the same size, as the minimap's picture does); glyph pages, uploaded again when their version changes. A batch whose image was never uploaded is skipped.
- **Pictures:** an `Offscreen` target the renderer draws into can be shown as an image too (`UiRenderer::set_picture`; the select screen's character). Its edges come out of the MSAA resolve with their colour already multiplied by their alpha, so its batches blend as premultiplied (one, one minus source alpha). Offscreen targets have the renderer's format, RGBA8 or BGRA8 (a window's); reading a BGRA8 one back swaps it to RGBA.
- **Sampling:** clamped to the texture's edge, and to its quad's part of it: the shader keeps each sample half a texel inside the quad's texture rectangle, so a piece of an atlas never takes in its neighbours, however it is filtered. Glyphs and pictures are drawn at their own size on whole pixels and sampled nearest. Images (the art, drawn larger in a scaled interface, `ui.md`, "Scale") are sampled as `UiFilter` says: `Smooth` (linear: soft, even at any scale) or `Sharp` (nearest: hard pixels, even only at whole scales). At a scale like 2.5 some pixel centres fall exactly between two texels; samples are shifted by a thousandth of a texel so nearest sampling picks the same one all along a row (without it the lines of a stretched gauge stepped up and down).
- **Clipping:** each batch's clip rectangle becomes the scissor, cut to the target; a batch clipped to nothing is skipped.
- `play` and `view ui` draw the interface into the same target as the world; `play --screenshot` reads the picture back after both. `play --no-interface` leaves the in-game windows out.

## Screenshots and the window
- **`openm2 view model <glb|gr2> [--part <glb|gr2>]… [--anim <glb|gr2>] [--time s]`:** loose files, or with `--assets <dir>` paths of an asset folder.
  - With `--screenshot out.png` it draws offscreen.
  - `--frames N` puts N poses spread over the animation in a grid (left to right, top to bottom; empty cells are background).
  - `--size WxH` sets the picture size (per pose).
  - `--yaw`/`--pitch` turn the camera.
  - `--texture NAME=PATH` replaces a texture by file name.
  - `--software` uses the software adapter.
- **Actors** (with `--assets`):
  - `view model <x.actor.toml>` draws the actor's base model.
  - `--shape N` draws shape N instead, and `--hair N` adds hair style N, each with its skins.
  - `--motion NAME` plays one of the actor's motions (`SET/NAME` to choose its set).
  - `--anim` also takes a motion file (`.motion.toml`), and plays the animation it names over the animation's own length. A different duration in the file is printed as a note.
  - An index or name that is not there is an error listing the ones there are.
  - A client's model and motion scripts (`.msm`, `.msa`) are refused: `openm2 assets import` makes actors and motion files of them.
- **Without `--screenshot`:** it opens a window that plays the animation. Drag turns the camera, the wheel zooms, Space pauses, the arrow keys step 1/30 s, R resets the camera, Escape closes.
- **`openm2 view map <map> --assets <dir>`:** a map of an asset folder (its folder, or its `map.toml`).
  - `--at X,Y` is the point looked at, in metres from the map's top left corner (east, south); without it, the middle of the map. The camera's target sits on the ground there (a floor over the terrain counts).
  - `--distance M` (metres) and `--yaw`/`--pitch` place the camera; yaw 0 looks north, pitch 90 looks straight down with north up. Without `--distance` the whole map is in view.
  - `--radius M` reads only the areas within M metres of `--at`; `--no-objects` leaves out buildings and effects; `--floors` draws the buildings' floors in magenta ([floors](#floors)); `--fog` draws the environment's fog; `--effect-time S` plays the map's effects S seconds before the picture.
  - With `--screenshot out.png` it draws offscreen (`--size`, default 1024×768); without, it opens a window: drag turns, the wheel zooms, WASD or the arrow keys move over the ground (Shift is faster), F switches the fog, R resets, Escape closes.
  - Straight down has no "up" on screen, so the pitch stops at 89.9°.
- **`openm2 view ui [LAYOUT…]`:** interface layouts, or one of `play`'s screens with `--screen`, over a plain background (`ui.md`, "Tools"), in a window or with `--screenshot`.
- **`openm2 view effect`:** an effect into a PNG ([above](#effects)); there is no window for effects yet.
- **Adapters:** the best adapter is used, hardware first. `WGPU_BACKEND` limits the backends tried. Without an adapter, `view model` fails with "no graphics adapter"; `play --screenshot` opens the adapter before it signs in, so without one it fails before reaching a server.
- **Screenshots of real models show the user's assets.** They stay on the user's machine (`local/` is gitignored), and `just no-assets` refuses picture files in the tree.

## Tests
- **CPU side:** `crates/m2-render/tests/cpu.rs` covers transforms, poses, parts, bounds, texture levels, coverage, framing and PNG.
- **GPU side:** `tests/gpu.rs` draws synthetic quads offscreen and reads pixels back: texture colour, white for missing textures, the palette moving vertices, alpha coverage, depth. Without an adapter it fails, as every test that draws does; a software one is enough (WARP on Windows, Mesa's lavapipe on Linux).
- **Terrain:** `tests/terrain.rs` builds area meshes from synthetic height, tile and water maps (places, normals, heights between vertices) and draws areas from above: the tiles' textures, the shadow map, missing texture numbers, water, instances.
- **Effects:** `tests/effect.rs` plays synthetic effects on the CPU: emission through the cycle and the particles' deaths, the cap on particles alive, loops, start times, direction × velocity and gravity, quads facing the camera, upright and flat, stretched along their step by the original's rule and standing up when still, an emitter's turning at each step, the spread, outward flight at 100, the same seed playing the same way, shapes and their edges, attached particles, colour and alpha over a life, texture animation, mesh frames with their visibility and loops, Bézier positions, draws joined by texture and blending. `tests/gpu.rs` draws effect triangles over a background (added, blended by alpha) and behind and in front of a model.
- **Interface:** `tests/ui.rs` draws a draw list over a target: an image's part, blending over the world, a clip rectangle, a batch whose image was never uploaded, a picture drawn by the renderer into a BGRA8 target, blended premultiplied and read back as RGBA, and an image's part drawn 8 times its size: sharp, its texels in blocks; smooth, blended between them, never with the image's other parts.
- **CLI:** `crates/m2-cli/tests/models.rs` runs `view model --screenshot` on synthetic files the same way; `tests/maps.rs` runs `map ls|info|check` and, once it is imported, `view map --screenshot` on a synthetic map (red and blue ground, a yellow roof, a green glow added to the ground by an effect object, and with `--floors` the roof model's floor in magenta); `floors.rs` and `mapview.rs` test floors placed and turned, the highest one, ramps, the triangles left out, the ground and a click onto a floor; `tests/effects.rs` runs `script check` and, once it is imported, `view effect --screenshot` on a synthetic effect (a red mesh square under a green particle); `tests/play.rs` runs `play --walk 0,2 --screenshot` against a scripted server on a synthetic client imported into an asset folder: it checks the move and wait messages, and with attributes walling off the way, the walk of each leg around the wall and the wait at its end, the run speed from the motion script, and Alice's red square in the middle of the picture with the wolf's blue one where the server put it (`--no-interface`: the interface would cover the small picture), then a wolf biting and another dying, each drawn 3 m off by its motion, with a `--say` line reaching the server, then `--fight`: the target, an attack move facing a wolf 2 m east and the hit its blade makes, byte for byte, and the wolf drawn fallen after the server's EntityDied, then `--pick-up` with two items on the ground (the bag a yellow square): the PickUp for the one beside Alice byte for byte, and Bob's left lying where the server put it, `play --screen select --screenshot` with Bob's red wall in his picture over the screen's background, and `play --screen login --screenshot` without a server; `tests/interface.rs` runs `view ui --screenshot` with fallback colours and with a synthetic client's TGA gauge and sub-image button imported, also twice the size with either filter.
